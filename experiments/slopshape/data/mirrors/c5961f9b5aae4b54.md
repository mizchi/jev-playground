# 5 Lessons Nonprofits Learned in a Year Like No Other

When the pandemic upended nearly every plan nonprofits had made, organizations everywhere were forced to rethink how they communicate, fundraise, host events, and support the people who power their missions. Galas were canceled. Volunteer shifts disappeared overnight. Staff members took on three jobs at once.

And yet, many nonprofits didn't just survive. They learned, adapted, and came out with a clearer sense of what really matters.

We asked Giveffect clients to share the biggest lessons they took away from this challenging year. Their answers were honest, insightful, and full of practical wisdom. Here are five lessons that stood out, along with advice for putting them into practice at your own organization.

## Lesson 1: Personalized Communication Makes All the Difference

> "We realized our supporters don't all want the same message. Segmenting our lists changed everything."

> "People responded when we spoke to them like individuals, not a mailing list."

When in-person connections disappeared, email, phone calls, and social media became the primary ways nonprofits stayed in touch with supporters. Many organizations quickly discovered that one-size-fits-all messaging wasn't cutting it.

A first-time donor, a longtime major gift supporter, a volunteer, and an event attendee all have different relationships with your organization. They care about different things and respond to different kinds of outreach.

**How to put it into practice:**

- **Segment your constituents.** Group supporters by giving history, engagement level, interests, location, or how they first connected with you.
- **Tailor your messages.** A lapsed donor might need a reminder of the impact they once made possible. A loyal monthly donor might appreciate a behind-the-scenes update.
- **Use personal details thoughtfully.** Addressing people by name and referencing their past involvement shows you know who they are.
- **Start small.** If segmentation feels overwhelming with a small team, begin with just two or three groups and build from there.

## Lesson 2: Plan Campaigns Early, and Leave Room to Revise

> "Starting our year-end campaign in the summer gave us breathing room when things inevitably changed."

> "We built in time to brainstorm and rework our plans. That flexibility saved us."

Last-minute campaigns are stressful in the best of times. In a year full of uncertainty, they became nearly impossible. Clients who started planning months in advance found they had the space to adjust when circumstances shifted.

Early planning doesn't mean locking everything in. It means creating a strong foundation with room for creativity and revision.

**How to put it into practice:**

- **Start three to six months out.** For major campaigns like year-end giving or annual appeals, begin planning well ahead of launch.
- **Schedule brainstorming sessions.** Bring together staff, board members, and even key volunteers to generate ideas.
- **Build in checkpoints.** Set regular points to review your plan and ask what needs to change.
- **Prepare backup plans.** Think through what you'll do if an event can't happen as planned or a key message doesn't land.
- **Map out your communications calendar.** Knowing what goes out when reduces last-minute scrambling for a stretched team.

## Lesson 3: Stay Flexible With Event Formats, and Put Safety First

> "Our virtual gala reached people we never would have seen in a ballroom."

> "We learned to plan for in-person, virtual, and hybrid all at once. It was a lot, but it worked."

> "Keeping our community safe had to come before everything else."

Events are a cornerstone of fundraising and community building for many nonprofits. When gatherings became unsafe, organizations got creative with virtual galas, online auctions, drive-through events, and hybrid formats.

Many clients were surprised to find that virtual events expanded their reach, bringing in supporters from across the country who could never have attended in person. Others found that small, safe, in-person moments helped maintain a sense of connection.

**How to put it into practice:**

- **Consider every format.** Evaluate whether your event could work virtually, in person, or as a hybrid.
- **Prioritize safety.** Follow public health guidance and communicate your safety measures clearly so attendees feel comfortable.
- **Invest in the virtual experience.** Good audio, engaging hosts, interactive elements, and a clear run of show make a big difference online.
- **Keep what works.** Even as in-person events return, virtual components can extend your reach and make participation more accessible.
- **Stay ready to pivot.** Have a plan for shifting formats if conditions change close to the event date.

## Lesson 4: Donor Retention Is the Heart of Fundraising

> "Our loyal donors carried us through. We should have been focusing on them all along."

> "It's so much easier to keep a donor than to find a new one."

With events canceled and new donor acquisition harder than ever, many nonprofits leaned on their existing supporters. Clients overwhelmingly reported that donor retention became their top priority, and many said they wished they had made it a focus sooner.

Retaining donors costs far less than acquiring new ones, and loyal donors often increase their giving over time. They're also more likely to become volunteers, advocates, and even legacy givers.

**How to put it into practice:**

- **Thank donors quickly and sincerely.** A prompt, personal thank-you sets the tone for a lasting relationship.
- **Show impact.** Share stories, photos, and updates that demonstrate how gifts are making a difference.
- **Stay in touch between asks.** Not every communication should be a request for money.
- **Track retention rates.** Know how many donors give again year over year, and set goals to improve.
- **Reach out to lapsed donors.** A thoughtful message can often reignite a relationship.
- **Encourage recurring giving.** Monthly donors provide steady, predictable support.

## Lesson 5: Support Volunteers With Flexibility and Genuine Appreciation

> "Letting volunteers choose their own shifts made a huge difference in how many showed up."

> "A simple, heartfelt thank-you meant more to our volunteers than we realized."

Volunteers faced their own challenges during the pandemic, from health concerns to caregiving responsibilities to shifting work schedules. Nonprofits that gave volunteers more control over their time and showed real appreciation kept their volunteer communities strong.

**How to put it into practice:**

- **Give volunteers schedule control.** Let them sign up for shifts that fit their lives, and make it easy to change plans.
- **Offer remote opportunities.** Virtual volunteering, such as making calls, writing notes, or helping with administrative tasks, can keep people engaged from home.
- **Acknowledge contributions sincerely.** Personalized thank-you notes, shout-outs on social media, and volunteer spotlights help people feel valued.
- **Check in regularly.** Ask volunteers how they're doing and what would make their experience better.
- **Celebrate milestones.** Recognize hours served, years of involvement, and special contributions.
