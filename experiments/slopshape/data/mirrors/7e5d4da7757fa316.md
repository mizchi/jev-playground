# How to Get Your Own Trucking Authority: A Step-by-Step Guide

There comes a point in many drivers' careers when working for someone else just doesn't cut it anymore. You want to choose your loads, set your own schedule, and keep more of what you earn. Running your own trucking operation offers that independence, but before you can haul freight for hire across state lines, you need operating authority from the federal government.

Getting your trucking authority can seem intimidating at first, with all the acronyms and forms involved. The good news is that it's a clear, manageable process if you take it one step at a time. Here's what you need to do.

## Step 1: Set Up Your Business

Before you file anything with the government, decide how your business will be structured. Many owner-operators form an LLC or corporation to separate personal assets from business liabilities. Register your business with your state, get an Employer Identification Number (EIN) from the IRS, and open a business bank account. These basics make everything that follows easier.

## Step 2: Register With the FMCSA

The Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration (FMCSA) oversees commercial trucking in the U.S. Registration now happens through the FMCSA's Unified Registration System (URS) online portal.

### File Form OP-1

The OP-1 is the application for motor carrier operating authority. On this form, you'll tell the FMCSA what kind of operation you're running, such as whether you're a for-hire carrier hauling general freight, household goods, or passengers. Be careful and accurate here. Mistakes can delay your application.

### File Form BOC-3

The BOC-3 designates a process agent in each state where you operate. A process agent is a person or company who can receive legal documents on your behalf. Most carriers use a blanket process agent service, which covers all 50 states for a modest one-time fee. The process agent usually files the BOC-3 for you electronically.

## Step 3: Get Your USDOT and MC Numbers

When you complete your registration, you'll receive two important identifiers:

- **USDOT Number:** This identifies your company and is used to track your safety record, inspections, and compliance. It's required for any commercial vehicle involved in interstate commerce.
- **MC Number:** Your Motor Carrier number is what grants you operating authority to haul regulated freight for hire across state lines.

Your USDOT number is issued right away, but your MC authority won't become active until the remaining requirements are met.

## Step 4: Buy the Right Insurance

Insurance is one of the biggest expenses for new carriers, and it's non-negotiable. The FMCSA requires minimum levels of public liability coverage, typically $750,000 for general freight, though many brokers and shippers expect at least $1,000,000. You'll likely also want cargo insurance, physical damage coverage, and possibly bobtail or non-trucking liability.

Your insurance provider must file proof of coverage with the FMCSA, usually through Form BMC-91 or BMC-91X. Shop around, compare quotes, and work with an agent who knows trucking. New authorities often pay higher premiums at first, so budget accordingly.

## Step 5: Set Up IRP Registration

If you'll be running in more than one state, you need International Registration Plan (IRP) registration. IRP lets you pay registration fees to multiple states and Canadian provinces based on the miles you drive in each, all through your home state. You'll receive apportioned plates and a cab card listing the jurisdictions you can operate in.

## Step 6: Open an IFTA Account

The International Fuel Tax Agreement (IFTA) simplifies fuel tax reporting for interstate carriers. Instead of filing fuel taxes separately in every state, you file one quarterly report with your base state, which then distributes the taxes to the other jurisdictions. You'll get IFTA decals for your truck. Keep detailed records of miles driven and fuel purchased in each state to make reporting smooth.

## Step 7: Enroll in a Drug and Alcohol Testing Program

Anyone operating a commercial motor vehicle requiring a CDL must be part of a DOT-compliant drug and alcohol testing program, even if you're a one-truck operation. Owner-operators typically join a consortium or third-party administrator (TPA) that manages random testing pools. You'll also need to register with the FMCSA Drug and Alcohol Clearinghouse and complete a pre-employment drug test before you start driving under your own authority.

## Step 8: Pay the Fee and Track Your Approval

There's a filing fee for each type of authority you apply for, currently $300 per authority. Once you've paid and submitted everything, the FMCSA will review your application.

You can track the status of your authority through the FMCSA's SAFER (Safety and Fitness Electronic Records) system. Look up your USDOT or MC number to see where things stand.

## Plan Ahead: How Long Does It Take?

This is where patience matters. After your application is submitted, there's a mandatory protest period, usually around 10 business days, during which anyone can object to your authority being granted. Your insurance and BOC-3 filings must also be received and processed.

All told, most new carriers should expect the process to take anywhere from three to six weeks, and sometimes longer if there are errors or backlogs. Don't quit your current job or book loads until your authority is officially active. Use the waiting period to line up brokers, set up your factoring or accounting systems, and get your truck ready.

## You Can Do This

Getting your trucking authority takes paperwork, money, and patience, but thousands of drivers do it every year. Take it one step at a time, double-check your forms, and keep good records. Before long, you'll be rolling down the highway as your own boss.
