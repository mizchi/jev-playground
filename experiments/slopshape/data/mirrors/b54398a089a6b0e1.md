# People Before Metrics: Rethinking Disaster Preparedness in Outsourcing

When Hurricane Otis slammed into Acapulco in October 2023, it caught nearly everyone off guard. In roughly half a day, the storm exploded from a tropical storm into a Category 5 hurricane, the strongest ever to make landfall on Mexico's Pacific coast. Homes were destroyed, power and communications went dark, and families were left searching for loved ones in the wreckage.

For businesses with teams in affected regions, storms like Otis raise urgent questions. How quickly can operations resume? How much service will be disrupted? What's the impact on service-level agreements?

These are fair questions. But they're not the first questions. The first question should always be: *Are our people safe?*

At Boldr, we believe disaster preparedness for outsourced operations has to start with people, not continuity metrics. When you take care of the humans behind the work, continuity follows. When you don't, no plan on paper will hold up for long.

## The Reality of Disruption in a Global Workforce

Global outsourcing gives companies access to extraordinary talent in places like the Philippines, Mexico, Kenya, South Africa, and beyond. It also means operating in regions exposed to a wide range of risks. Understanding them is the first step toward preparing responsibly.

### Natural Disasters

Some of the world's most vibrant outsourcing hubs are also among the most disaster-prone. The WorldRiskIndex, which assesses countries' exposure and vulnerability to extreme natural events, has repeatedly ranked the Philippines as the highest-risk country in the world. Mexico also sits near the top of the list.

The Philippines experiences an average of around 20 typhoons a year. Typhoon Haiyan (known locally as Yolanda) killed more than 6,000 people in 2013, and Typhoon Rai (Odette) devastated large parts of the Visayas and Mindanao in December 2021, knocking out power and connectivity for weeks in some areas. The country also sits on the Pacific Ring of Fire, facing earthquakes and volcanic eruptions such as the 2020 eruption of Taal Volcano near Manila.

Mexico, too, faces a mix of hazards: hurricanes on both coasts, flooding, and powerful earthquakes like the magnitude 7.1 quake that struck central Mexico in September 2017, killing hundreds and damaging buildings across Mexico City.

With climate change increasing the intensity of extreme weather events, these risks are growing, not shrinking.

### Political Unrest

Political instability can disrupt operations just as severely as a storm. The military coup in Myanmar in 2021 led to internet shutdowns, protests, and violence that upended daily life and business. In 2022, Sri Lanka's economic crisis sparked mass protests, fuel shortages, and power cuts. Elections, civil demonstrations, and sudden policy changes can all lead to curfews, transportation disruptions, or communications blackouts in countries around the world.

### Industrial Accidents

Some disruptions are man-made and entirely unexpected. The 2020 explosion at the port of Beirut killed more than 200 people, injured thousands, and damaged large parts of the city. The 2013 collapse of the Rana Plaza building in Bangladesh killed more than 1,100 garment workers and exposed how fragile, and how human, global supply chains really are. Fires, chemical spills, and infrastructure failures can strike without warning, affecting whole communities.

## Why People-First Preparedness Matters

Traditional business continuity planning often focuses on systems: backup servers, redundant internet connections, alternative sites, and recovery time objectives. These things matter, and we invest in them. But they miss something essential.

Every outsourced operation is made up of people who have families, homes, and communities. When disaster strikes, those people are dealing with the same crisis as everyone around them. Asking them to log in before they know whether their family is safe, or while their home is flooded, isn't just unrealistic. It's wrong.

A people-first approach recognizes that the well-being of team members is the foundation of any sustainable continuity plan. Teams who feel supported during a crisis are more likely to return to work quickly, stay with the company long-term, and deliver exceptional work for clients. Care builds resilience.

As a certified B Corporation, Boldr is committed to balancing purpose and profit, and to considering the impact of our decisions on workers, communities, and the environment. Disaster preparedness is one of the places where those values are tested most.

## How Boldr Puts People First in Business Continuity

Here's a look at the practices that shape how we prepare for, and respond to, disruption.

### Employee Check-Ins and Tracking

When a major storm, earthquake, or other emergency hits, our first priority is reaching every team member. We run structured check-ins to confirm each person's safety and understand their situation, whether they have power, internet, shelter, and access to essentials.

We track responses closely, following up with anyone we haven't heard from and escalating when needed. This gives our leaders a clear, real-time picture of who is safe, who needs help, and who is in a position to work. Only then do we turn to operational planning.

### Support for Affected Staff

Knowing someone needs help is only the first step. We provide practical support for team members affected by disasters, which may include emergency financial assistance, relief goods, help with temporary accommodation, and flexibility around time off.

We also recognize that the effects of a disaster don't end when the storm passes. Rebuilding a home, caring for family, or coping with trauma can take weeks or months. We try to give our people the time and support they need to recover, with access to mental health resources where possible.

### Training and Certifications

Preparedness starts well before a disaster. We invest in training that helps team members respond safely and confidently in an emergency, including first aid, emergency response, and evacuation procedures. Our leaders are trained in crisis management, and we continuously review our business continuity practices against recognized standards.

When people know what to do, they're safer, calmer, and better able to help each other and their communities.

### Clear, Honest Client Communication

Our clients deserve transparency, especially during a crisis. We keep them informed from the moment we become aware of a potential threat, sharing what we know, what we're doing to keep our teams safe, and what the likely impact on service will be.

We're honest when there will be disruptions, and we work with clients on realistic plans to manage them. In our experience, clients who understand and share our values appreciate a partner who puts people first. Many have gone out of their way to support affected teams themselves.

### Debriefs and Continuous Improvement

After every significant event, we hold debriefs to review what went well and what could be improved. We gather feedback from team members, leaders, and clients, and use those lessons to strengthen our plans.

No two disasters are the same, and preparedness is never finished. Each event makes us a little wiser and a little more ready for the next one.

### Cross-Country Talent Redundancy

One of the most effective ways to protect both people and operations is to avoid putting all of a client's work in one place. With teams across multiple countries, Boldr can distribute work so that if one location is affected, colleagues elsewhere can help carry the load.

This redundancy means no one has to choose between their family's safety and their job. Team members in an affected area can focus on recovery while their counterparts in another country keep critical work moving. It's continuity built on shared responsibility, not pressure.

## The Wider Impact: Communities, Not Just Companies

Outsourcing done right can be a powerful force for good. It creates meaningful jobs, supports local economies, and helps families build more secure futures. But that impact is only real if companies show up for their people when it matters most.

When disasters strike, businesses have a choice. They can treat affected regions as risks to be managed, or as communities to be supported. At Boldr, we choose the latter. We've seen our teams organize relief drives, volunteer in recovery efforts, and support neighbors in need. When companies invest in their people, that care ripples outward into the wider community.

We believe the outsourcing industry as a whole has an opportunity, and a responsibility, to lead here. Continuity metrics will always matter. But they should be the outcome of a thoughtful, human-centered approach, not a substitute for one.
