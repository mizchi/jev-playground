# From Product-Centric to Customer-Centric: How Industrial Manufacturers Can Unlock Cross-Sell Growth With Better Data

For decades, industrial machinery and equipment manufacturers have organized themselves around what they make. Business units are built around product lines, sales teams are compensated on specific portfolios, and customer relationships are often managed in silos. That model has served the industry well, but it leaves significant revenue on the table.

The customer who buys a line of compressors from one division may also need pumps, service contracts, replacement parts, and digital monitoring solutions from three others. Too often, no one in the organization has a complete picture of that customer, and the cross-sell opportunity goes unnoticed.

For manufacturing executives, the question is no longer whether to become more customer-centric. It is how to make the transition without disrupting the business. The answer starts with data.

## The Power of Combining Internal and External Customer Data

Most manufacturers already hold a wealth of customer information. It lives in ERP systems, CRM platforms, service management tools, dealer portals, and warranty databases. The problem is that each system tells only part of the story, and the same customer may appear dozens of times under slightly different names, addresses, and account numbers.

When that internal data is consolidated and enriched with external sources, such as firmographic data, corporate hierarchies, industry classifications, and installed-base intelligence, a far clearer picture emerges. Manufacturers can see:

- Which customers buy from multiple business units and which buy from only one
- How parent companies and subsidiaries relate across regions
- Where competitors' equipment is installed at customer sites
- Which accounts show buying patterns that signal readiness for complementary products

This unified view turns cross-selling from a matter of individual relationships into a repeatable, data-driven strategy.

## The Hurdles Executives Must Plan For

The transition is rarely simple. In our work with industrial manufacturers, four challenges consistently surface.

**Building trustworthy golden records.** A customer-centric strategy depends on a single, authoritative record for each customer. Creating that golden record requires matching, merging, and validating data from many sources. If sales teams do not trust the data, they will not use it.

**Overcoming cultural resistance to centralization.** Business units that have long owned their customer relationships may view a centralized customer master as a loss of control. Executive sponsorship and clear communication about shared benefits are essential.

**Crediting product business units fairly.** When one division identifies a cross-sell opportunity for another, who gets credit? Without clear attribution and incentive models, collaboration stalls. Organizations must define how revenue and opportunities are shared.

**Sustaining data stewardship.** Master data is not a one-time project. Customers merge, relocate, and restructure. Without ongoing governance, defined ownership, and stewardship processes, data quality erodes and the golden record loses value.

## Client Engagement: A Large Equipment Manufacturer

A global manufacturer of heavy equipment approached Infoverity with a familiar problem. Its product divisions operated independently, each maintaining its own customer data. Leadership suspected significant overlap among customers but had no reliable way to confirm it.

Infoverity conducted a roadmapping engagement that assessed data across multiple ERP and CRM systems. The team designed a customer master data strategy, including matching rules, external data enrichment, and a hierarchy model linking subsidiaries to parent accounts. The analysis revealed that a substantial share of customers purchased from only one division, despite having needs aligned with others.

With a clear roadmap and governance model in place, the manufacturer was positioned to launch coordinated account strategies and give sales teams visibility into cross-division opportunities.

## Client Engagement: An Oilfield Services Firm

An oilfield services company faced a different version of the same challenge. Its customer data was fragmented across service lines and geographies, making it difficult to understand the full scope of its relationships with major operators.

Infoverity helped the firm define a unified customer data model and integrate external data to clarify corporate hierarchies across operators, joint ventures, and regional entities. The engagement also established stewardship processes and ownership to keep data accurate as the customer landscape changed.

The result was a consolidated view of customer relationships that enabled leadership to identify underserved accounts and pursue bundled service offerings with greater confidence.

## Making the Shift

Moving from product-centric to customer-centric selling is as much an organizational change as a technical one. It requires trusted data, aligned incentives, and sustained governance. Manufacturers that invest in these foundations can uncover hidden revenue, strengthen customer relationships, and compete more effectively in an increasingly demanding market.
