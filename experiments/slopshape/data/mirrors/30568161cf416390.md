# How to Write a Job Description That Attracts the Right Candidates (and Filters Out the Wrong Ones)

When unemployment is high, posting a job can feel like opening the floodgates. Hundreds of applications pour in within days, and suddenly you're spending hours sifting through resumes that don't come close to what you need.

The good news? A well-written job description is one of your best tools for managing applicant volume. It attracts qualified candidates, helps poor fits self-select out, and saves you time at every stage of the hiring process. Here's how to write one that works.

## Start Before You Write

Before you draft a single sentence, get clear on what the role actually requires. Take some time to define:

- **Responsibilities:** What will this person do day to day, week to week, and month to month?
- **Success measures:** What does great performance look like after 30, 90, and 180 days?
- **Skills:** Which technical and soft skills are essential to doing the job well?
- **Culture fit:** What kind of work environment will this person thrive in? Fast-paced and independent? Collaborative and structured?

Talk to the people who will work with this hire. Their input will help you describe the role accurately and avoid surprises later.

## Weigh Potential Against Experience

It's tempting to ask for years of experience in exactly the same role. But experience isn't always the best predictor of success. Candidates with strong transferable skills, a track record of learning quickly, and genuine enthusiasm can often outperform those who simply check the "years of experience" box.

Hiring for potential also widens your talent pool. Strict experience requirements can unintentionally screen out career changers, returning parents, veterans, and people from underrepresented backgrounds who haven't had the same access to traditional career paths. Opening the door to potential can bring fresh perspectives and meaningful diversity to your team.

## Separate Must-Haves from Nice-to-Haves

A long list of requirements can discourage great candidates, particularly those who tend not to apply unless they meet every single qualification. Be honest about what's truly essential.

Split your qualifications into two clear sections:

- **Must-haves:** The non-negotiable skills, certifications, or experience required to do the job.
- **Nice-to-haves:** Bonus qualifications that would be helpful but can be learned on the job.

This clarity helps qualified candidates apply with confidence and helps unqualified candidates recognize that the role isn't the right fit.

## Sell the Company and the Role

Even in a competitive job market, the best candidates have options. Your job description is a marketing document as much as a list of requirements.

Tell candidates why they'd want to work for you. Highlight your mission, your team, growth opportunities, benefits, flexibility, and what makes your culture unique. Explain how this role contributes to the bigger picture. When candidates can see themselves thriving in the position, you'll attract people who are genuinely excited, not just looking for any job.

## Watch for Gendered Language

Words matter. Certain terms can subtly signal that a role is meant for one gender over another. Words like "rockstar," "ninja," "aggressive," or "dominant" may discourage some candidates, while overly soft language can have the opposite effect.

Aim for neutral, inclusive wording. Use "you" to address the candidate directly, and describe the work itself rather than personality stereotypes. There are free online tools that can scan your job description for gender-coded language and suggest alternatives.

## Keep It Concise and Well-Structured

Candidates skim. Make it easy for them to find the information they need by using a clear structure:

1. **Job title:** Clear and searchable. Skip the creative titles.
2. **Summary:** A few sentences about the role and why it matters.
3. **Responsibilities:** A focused bullet list of key duties.
4. **Qualifications:** Must-haves and nice-to-haves.
5. **About the company:** Your mission, culture, and benefits.
6. **How to apply:** Clear next steps.

Keep paragraphs short and cut the jargon. A focused description is easier to read and more likely to attract serious applicants.

## Gather Feedback and Proofread

Before you post, share the draft with a few trusted colleagues, ideally including someone currently in a similar role. Ask them: Does this accurately describe the job? Would it excite you? Is anything confusing or missing?

Then proofread carefully. Typos and errors can make your company look careless and may turn off detail-oriented candidates.

## Add Pre-Screening Questions

When you expect a high volume of applicants, pre-screening questions are a lifesaver. Add a few short questions to your application that address your must-haves, such as:

- "Do you have a valid certification in X?"
- "Are you able to work on-site three days per week?"
- "Describe a time you managed a project from start to finish."

These questions help you quickly identify qualified candidates and filter out those who don't meet key requirements, so you can focus your time on the most promising applicants.
