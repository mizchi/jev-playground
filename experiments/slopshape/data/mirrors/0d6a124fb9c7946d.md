# How a Diesel Fuel Card Takes the Busywork Out of Running a Trucking Operation

Whether you drive your own rig as an owner-operator or manage a fleet of hundreds of trucks, you already know that fuel is one of your biggest expenses. What often gets overlooked is how much time goes into managing it. Collecting receipts, reconciling statements, checking for unauthorized purchases, calculating fuel taxes by state, and preparing reports for your accountant can eat up hours every week.

A diesel fuel card is designed to lift much of that burden. The right program doesn't just help you save at the pump. It gives you better control over spending, cleaner records, and easier tax compliance, so you can spend less time on paperwork and more time keeping your trucks moving.

Here's a closer look at how a fuel card can simplify your operation and what to consider when choosing one.

## Fuel Cards Work for Operations of Every Size

It's a common misconception that fuel cards are only for large fleets. In reality, they can benefit almost any trucking business.

**Owner-operators** gain a simple way to separate business fuel from personal spending, keep track of every transaction, and generate the records they need at tax time without digging through a glove box full of receipts.

**Small and mid-sized fleets** get visibility into what each driver is spending and where, along with tools to prevent misuse before it happens.

**Large fleets** can centralize fuel management across dozens or hundreds of vehicles, apply consistent policies, and pull detailed reports that support budgeting and cost analysis.

No matter your size, the goal is the same: less time chasing paper and more confidence that your fuel dollars are being spent the way you intended.

## Cardlock vs. Universal Cards: Understanding Your Options

Not all fuel cards work the same way. The two main types are cardlock programs and universal fuel cards, and each has its strengths.

### Cardlock Programs

Cardlock fuel cards are used at dedicated fueling sites, often unattended stations built with commercial vehicles in mind. These programs frequently offer pricing based on wholesale or cost-plus rates rather than retail pump prices, which can translate into meaningful savings on high-volume diesel purchases.

The trade-off is coverage. Cardlock networks generally have fewer locations than retail station networks. If your trucks run regular routes near cardlock sites, this can be an excellent fit. If your drivers cover unpredictable territory, you may find yourself with gaps.

### Universal Fuel Cards

Universal fuel cards are accepted at a broad network of fuel stations, including major brands and truck stops. That flexibility makes them well suited to long-haul operations and fleets that operate across wide regions. Drivers can fuel up almost anywhere, which reduces out-of-route miles and downtime spent hunting for an approved station.

Pricing on universal cards is usually tied to retail prices, sometimes with rebates or discounts per gallon.

### Look Beyond the Discount

It's tempting to pick a fuel card based on the advertised discount alone. But a few cents off per gallon doesn't mean much if it's eaten up by fees. Before signing up, review the program's full fee structure, including:

- Monthly or annual card fees
- Transaction fees
- Out-of-network fees
- Late payment fees and finance charges
- Fees for reports or additional services

Compare what you'll actually pay under realistic usage. The best program is the one that delivers the lowest total cost and the features you need, not necessarily the one with the biggest headline discount.

## Driver Spending Controls and Alerts

One of the biggest advantages of a fuel card is the ability to set rules around how it's used. Instead of trusting that every purchase is legitimate, you can build safeguards right into the card.

Common controls include:

- **Purchase type restrictions**, limiting cards to diesel only or allowing specific additional items such as DEF
- **Daily, weekly, or per-transaction limits** on gallons or dollar amounts
- **Time-of-day and day-of-week restrictions**
- **Location restrictions** to keep purchases within certain regions
- **Driver ID or PIN requirements** so every transaction is tied to a specific driver and vehicle

Alerts add another layer of protection. You can be notified when a transaction falls outside your set parameters, such as an unusually large purchase or fueling at an odd hour. That means you can address potential issues quickly rather than discovering them weeks later on a statement.

## Route Planning Made Easier

Many fuel card programs include a mobile app that helps drivers find in-network stations along their route. Drivers can see locations, check fuel prices, and plan stops in advance, which helps them avoid expensive off-network purchases and unnecessary detours.

For fleet managers, this means fewer surprises and more consistent fuel costs. For drivers, it means less guesswork on the road.

## Automatic IFTA Tax Tracking

If your trucks cross state lines, you know how time-consuming International Fuel Tax Agreement reporting can be. You have to track gallons purchased and miles driven in each jurisdiction, then file quarterly returns.

A fuel card can make this much simpler by automatically recording where every gallon is purchased, broken down by state. Instead of compiling receipts manually, you'll have organized data ready to support your IFTA filings. That reduces errors, saves hours each quarter, and gives you a clear record if you're ever audited.

## Off-Road Fuel Tax Deductions for Dual-Purpose Equipment

Some trucks and equipment use fuel for both on-road driving and off-road purposes, such as powering a refrigeration unit, a pump, a power take-off, or other auxiliary equipment. Fuel used for qualifying off-road purposes may be eligible for tax refunds or credits.

Tracking that usage by hand is difficult. With the right fuel card program, fuel purchases can be categorized and documented to help you identify and support these off-road fuel tax deductions. Over time, that can add up to real savings you might otherwise miss.

## Custom Accounting Reports

Every business tracks its finances a little differently. Fuel card programs typically offer reporting tools that let you view and export data in the format that works best for you.

You can generate reports by driver, vehicle, department, location, date range, or fuel type. Many programs let you export data directly into accounting software, cutting down on manual data entry and making month-end reconciliation faster and more accurate. Clear reports also make it easier to spot trends, such as a truck whose fuel economy is declining or a driver whose spending is out of line with others.

## California Agricultural Sales Tax Exemption

For operations in California's agricultural sector, fuel cards can also help with sales tax savings. California offers a partial sales and use tax exemption on diesel fuel used in farming activities and food processing. Qualifying businesses can apply the exemption to eligible purchases, and a fuel card program that supports this can help ensure the exemption is applied and documented correctly.

If your business qualifies, this can make a noticeable difference in your fuel costs over the course of a year.

## Less Busywork, More Oversight

Running a trucking operation involves enough challenges without spending your evenings sorting receipts and building spreadsheets. A diesel fuel card brings your fuel spending into one place, gives you control over how drivers use it, and simplifies tax tracking and reporting.

The key is choosing a program that fits the way your business actually operates. Consider where your trucks travel, how much fuel you buy, which controls you need, and what the program really costs once all fees are included. With the right card in hand, you'll have tighter oversight, cleaner records, and more time to focus on growing your business.
