# Closing the Preventive Care Gap: Why Navigation Support Is the Missing Link for Medicare Advantage Members

Medicare Advantage plans have invested heavily in preventive benefits. The Annual Wellness Visit (AWV) is covered at no cost to members, screenings are readily available, and outreach campaigns remind members to schedule their appointments. And yet, for many plans, AWV completion rates remain stubbornly low.

For plan administrators, this can be puzzling. If the benefit is free and the value is clear, why aren't more members using it?

The answer lies less in the benefit itself and more in the everyday realities of older adults' lives. For many members, the path from "you're eligible" to "you've completed your visit" is full of obstacles. Closing that gap requires more than reminders. It requires navigation.

## The Real Barriers to Preventive Care

**Underestimating the value of prevention.** Many older adults see the doctor only when something is wrong. If they feel fine, an annual wellness visit can seem unnecessary. Some confuse the AWV with a full physical and assume they don't need one, while others simply don't understand how preventive care can catch problems before they become serious.

**Physical and cognitive limitations.** Mobility challenges, chronic pain, vision or hearing loss, and early cognitive decline can make scheduling and attending appointments difficult. A member who struggles to remember appointments, or who can no longer drive, may quietly let preventive care slide.

**Confusion over benefits.** Medicare Advantage plans often include a wide range of benefits, but members don't always understand what's covered, what it costs, or how to access it. Fear of unexpected bills keeps many members from seeking care they're entitled to receive at no charge.

**Limited digital literacy.** As health care increasingly moves online, from patient portals to appointment scheduling apps to telehealth, members who aren't comfortable with technology can be left behind. Many older adults lack reliable internet access or the confidence to navigate digital tools on their own.

## The Cost of Missed Prevention

These barriers aren't just inconvenient; they're expensive. When preventive visits are skipped, chronic conditions like diabetes, hypertension, heart disease, and COPD often go undiagnosed or poorly managed. Small, treatable issues escalate into complications requiring emergency care, hospital admissions, and readmissions.

For plans, the financial impact is significant. Avoidable hospitalizations and emergency visits drive up medical costs, while gaps in preventive care affect quality measures and Star Ratings, which in turn influence reimbursement and member enrollment. For members, the cost is measured in declining health, reduced independence, and lower quality of life.

## How Companion Care Closes the Gap

Companion care professionals provide the personal, hands-on support that bridges the distance between benefit design and real-world use. Offered as a supplemental benefit, companion care helps members overcome the specific barriers that keep them from preventive care.

**Helping members understand their coverage.** Companions can walk members through their benefits in plain language, explaining that the AWV is covered and what to expect during the visit. This simple conversation can ease fears about cost and build confidence to take the next step.

**Scheduling appointments.** Rather than leaving members to navigate phone trees and online portals alone, companions help make calls, schedule visits, and keep track of upcoming appointments with reminders.

**Arranging transportation.** Transportation remains one of the most common reasons older adults miss appointments. Companions can help coordinate rides, whether through plan-covered transportation benefits or other resources, and in some cases accompany members to their visits.

**Supporting telehealth access.** For members who can benefit from virtual visits, companions help set up devices, log in to appointments, and troubleshoot technical issues, turning telehealth from a source of frustration into a convenient option.

**Serving as the plan's eyes and ears.** Because companions spend regular time with members, they often notice changes others might miss: a new difficulty walking, confusion about medications, an empty refrigerator, or signs of loneliness and depression. With the right communication channels, these observations can be shared with the plan, enabling timely care management interventions before a small issue becomes a crisis.

## Value for Plans and Members Alike

Health care navigation support through companion care creates value on both sides. For plans, it means higher AWV completion rates, better management of chronic conditions, fewer avoidable hospitalizations, improved quality scores, and stronger member satisfaction and retention.

For members, it means something even more personal: someone in their corner who helps them understand their options, get the care they need, and stay healthy and independent at home.

Preventive care works best when members can actually access it. By pairing strong benefits with human support, Medicare Advantage plans can finally close the gap between what's available and what's used, and deliver better outcomes for everyone.
