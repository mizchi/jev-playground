# How to Choose the Right Size Urn for Your Loved One's Ashes

Choosing an urn is often one of the first decisions a family makes after a loss, and it can feel overwhelming. You may be navigating grief, managing arrangements, and suddenly facing questions you've never had to think about before: How much space do ashes take up? Should we keep everything together or share it? What if we want to scatter some and keep some close?

Please know that there's no wrong way to honor someone you love. This guide is here to walk you gently through the options, from full-size urns to small pieces of cremation jewelry, so you can make a choice that feels right for your family.

## Why More Families Are Choosing Cremation

Cremation has become the most common choice for families in the United States and in many other countries. The reasons are varied and deeply personal. For some, it's about cost, since cremation is typically less expensive than a traditional burial. For others, it's about flexibility. Cremation allows families to hold a memorial service when everyone can gather, to scatter ashes in a meaningful place, or to keep a loved one close at home.

Changing attitudes play a part, too. Many people today feel less tied to traditional burial customs and more drawn to memorials that reflect the unique life of the person they've lost. Cremation opens the door to that kind of personalization, and the urn you choose becomes a central part of it.

## Understanding the Basics: How Much Space Do Ashes Need?

The most widely used guideline in the memorial industry is simple: **plan for about one cubic inch of urn capacity for every pound of healthy body weight** before passing. So a person who weighed 180 pounds would generally need an urn with a capacity of around 180 cubic inches.

It's always wise to round up. A little extra space makes transferring ashes easier and gives peace of mind. If you're unsure, your funeral home or crematory can usually tell you the approximate volume of the remains they've returned to you.

With that in mind, let's look at urn sizes from largest to smallest.

## Standard Adult and Extra-Large Urns

**Typical capacity:** 180 to 250 cubic inches for standard adult urns; 250 cubic inches and up for extra-large urns

**Who they suit:** Families who want to keep all of their loved one's ashes together in one place

A standard adult urn is designed to hold the full cremated remains of most adults. These urns commonly stand around 9 to 12 inches tall, though the shape can vary widely, from classic vase forms to boxes, sculptural designs, and even biodegradable options for burial or water ceremonies.

Extra-large urns are intended for individuals who were taller or heavier, or for those who simply want extra room. They're also used as **companion urns**, which hold the ashes of two people, often spouses or partners who wished to rest together. Companion urns typically offer 400 cubic inches or more and may feature one shared chamber or two separate compartments.

If you plan to place the urn in a columbarium niche or bury it in a cemetery, check the niche dimensions or cemetery requirements before you buy. Some cemeteries also require an urn vault for burial.

## Medium Urns

**Typical capacity:** Roughly 40 to 150 cubic inches

**Who they suit:** Small children, petite adults, beloved pets, or family members sharing a portion of ashes

Medium urns fill a meaningful space between full-size and keepsake urns. For families who have lost an infant or a young child, a medium urn often offers the right capacity in a form that feels gentle and proportionate. Many designs in this range are softer in style, with delicate engravings or motifs that reflect a little one's spirit.

Medium urns are also a thoughtful choice when ashes will be shared. For example, if a surviving spouse keeps the majority of the ashes, an adult child might choose a medium urn to keep a substantial portion at their own home. Many families also use medium urns when some ashes will be scattered and the remainder kept.

## Tealight Urns

**Typical capacity:** Usually 3 to 15 cubic inches

**Who they suit:** Anyone who wants a small portion of ashes held in a comforting, everyday memorial

Tealight urns combine a small, sealed compartment for ashes with a holder for a tealight or small candle. They're a lovely way to create a quiet moment of remembrance. Lighting the candle on a birthday, an anniversary, or simply at the end of a hard day can offer a sense of connection.

Because they hold only a small amount, tealight urns work beautifully alongside a full-size urn or as part of a set distributed among family members. They also sit comfortably on a mantel, bookshelf, or bedside table.

## Keepsake Urns

**Typical capacity:** Generally 1 to 10 cubic inches

**Who they suit:** Families dividing ashes among several people, or anyone who wants a small, personal memorial

Keepsake urns are miniature versions of traditional urns, and they're one of the most popular ways to share ashes. If your family is spread across different cities, or several children and grandchildren each want something to hold on to, keepsakes allow everyone to keep a part of your loved one nearby.

Many full-size urn designs come with **matching keepsake sets**. This is a wonderful option for families who want a sense of unity: the main urn can stay with one family member while matching keepsakes go to others. Each piece looks the same, a quiet reminder that everyone is connected by the same love and the same loss.

Keepsakes are also a practical choice when most ashes will be scattered. Keeping a small portion in a keepsake means you'll always have a place to return to, even if the rest of the ashes rest in the ocean, a garden, or a favorite mountain trail.

## Cremation Jewelry

**Typical capacity:** A tiny amount, often just a pinch of ashes

**Who they suit:** Those who want to carry their loved one with them every day

Cremation jewelry is the smallest way to keep ashes, and for many people it's the most intimate. Pendants, bracelets, rings, and charms are designed with a small, sealed chamber that holds a pinch of ashes. Some families also choose pieces that hold a lock of hair, a bit of soil from a special place, or dried flowers from the service.

Styles range from simple hearts and teardrops to modern bars, cylinders, and custom-engraved pieces. Materials often include stainless steel, sterling silver, and gold. Because each piece holds so little, cremation jewelry is easy to share, and many families choose several pieces so that each person who wants one can have one.

A helpful tip: most cremation jewelry comes with a small funnel and a screw that can be sealed with a bit of adhesive to keep the ashes secure. Filling can feel emotional, so you may want to do it together or ask a funeral director for help.

## Combining Sizes to Meet Your Family's Needs

You don't have to choose just one type of urn. Many families find comfort in a combination, such as:

- A **full-size urn** for the primary caregiver or spouse
- **Keepsake urns** for each adult child
- **Cremation jewelry** for grandchildren or close friends
- A **tealight urn** for a shared family space

When planning, start with the total volume of ashes, then subtract the amount going into smaller keepsakes and jewelry. The remaining amount will guide your choice for the main urn, or tell you how much will be available for scattering.

## A Gentle Next Step: Talking as a Family

Before choosing an urn, it can help to sit down together and talk openly about what everyone hopes for. A few questions to guide the conversation:

- Did your loved one express any wishes about their ashes?
- Will the ashes be kept together, shared among family members, or scattered?
- Where will the urn be kept, and who will care for it?
- Are there any cemetery, niche, or travel requirements to consider?

These conversations can be tender, and it's okay to take your time. There's no deadline for deciding what to do with ashes, and many families keep them safely in a temporary container until they feel ready.

Whatever you choose, remember that the urn is simply a vessel. What truly matters is the love it represents and the memories you'll carry forward. If you have questions along the way, our team is always here to help you find a memorial that feels right.
