# 4 Overlooked Ways to Improve Member Experience (and Why Your Star Ratings Will Thank You)

Let's be honest: health plans aren't exactly famous for delighting their customers. When people think of great customer experience, they tend to think of the coffee shop that remembers their order or the online retailer that ships overnight. Their health plan? Probably not the first thing that comes to mind.

That's a problem, because member experience isn't a soft, nice-to-have metric. It drives satisfaction scores, influences CAHPS results, shapes Medicare Advantage star ratings, and ultimately affects retention, bonus payments, and the bottom line. Members who feel supported are more likely to stay, more likely to engage in preventive care, and less likely to end up in the emergency room.

If your organization is still catching up on customer-centered practices, you're not alone. The good news is that some of the most effective improvements aren't the obvious ones. Here are four less obvious levers worth pulling.

## 1. Personalize Benefits Around the Whole Person

Members aren't just a collection of diagnosis codes. They're people with jobs, families, stressors, and living situations that have a huge impact on their health. A member managing diabetes who lives alone and can't get to the grocery store has very different needs than a member with the same diagnosis who has a supportive family nearby.

Whole-person care means looking beyond clinical needs to the social determinants of health: food access, transportation, housing, loneliness, and caregiving demands. When benefits reflect those realities, members notice.

**Tactics to try:**

- **Ask better questions during onboarding.** Health risk assessments can capture more than medical history. Include questions about social support, transportation, and daily challenges.
- **Use data to segment members by need, not just demographics.** Two 70-year-olds can have wildly different lives.
- **Offer flexible supplemental benefits.** Meal delivery, companionship, help around the house, and transportation can make a bigger difference than another brochure.
- **Address loneliness directly.** Social isolation is linked to worse health outcomes and higher costs. Programs like Papa connect members with Papa Pals who provide one-on-one companionship and help with everyday tasks.
- **Support caregivers, too.** Family caregivers are often the ones navigating a member's care. Giving them resources helps everyone.

## 2. Help Members Decode Their Coverage

Deductibles. Coinsurance. Out-of-pocket maximums. Prior authorization. Explanation of benefits. If you work in health insurance, these terms are second nature. For most members, they might as well be written in ancient Greek.

Confusion breeds frustration. Members who don't understand their coverage are more likely to skip care, rack up unexpected bills, and call your service center in a less-than-cheerful mood. Clarity is one of the cheapest and most underrated ways to improve experience.

**Tactics to try:**

- **Rewrite member materials in plain language.** Aim for a reading level most people can comfortably manage. If your summary of benefits needs a glossary, it needs a rewrite.
- **Use real-life examples.** Show members what a typical doctor's visit, prescription, or ER trip will actually cost under their plan.
- **Proactively explain changes.** When benefits or formularies change at the start of the year, reach out before members find out at the pharmacy counter.
- **Train service reps to teach, not just answer.** A quick explanation of how a deductible works can prevent five future calls.
- **Create short videos or visual guides.** Sometimes a 60-second explainer beats a 40-page PDF. (Shocking, we know.)

## 3. Pair Digital Tools With Human Support

There's a tendency to assume that "modern" member experience means pushing everyone toward an app. Digital tools are great, and many members love them. But plenty of others, especially older adults, prefer picking up the phone or talking to a real person. And even digital natives want a human when things get complicated.

The best approach isn't digital-first or phone-first. It's member-first, with options that meet people where they are.

**Tactics to try:**

- **Offer multiple channels and make them consistent.** Members should be able to get the same accurate answer whether they use the app, the website, chat, or the phone.
- **Don't bury the phone number.** Hiding human support to push digital adoption tends to backfire on satisfaction.
- **Help members learn digital tools.** Some members would use the portal if someone showed them how. Papa Pals can help members get comfortable with telehealth visits, apps, and online portals.
- **Design for different generations.** Younger members may want text reminders and self-service; older members may value a friendly call. Let members set their preferences.
- **Use digital data to trigger human outreach.** If a member hasn't refilled a prescription or logged in for months, that's a cue for a personal check-in.

## 4. Get Creative With Preventive Care Engagement

Everyone agrees that preventive care saves money and improves outcomes. Annual wellness visits, screenings, vaccines, and chronic condition management are the bread and butter of quality measures. Yet getting members to actually show up remains a stubborn challenge.

Reminder letters alone aren't cutting it. Engaging members in preventive care takes a little more imagination and a lot more understanding of the barriers they face.

**Tactics to try:**

- **Remove practical barriers.** Lack of transportation is one of the most common reasons people miss appointments. Papa can provide rides to and from appointments, along with a friendly companion for the trip.
- **Bring care closer to home.** In-home screenings, mobile clinics, and telehealth visits make it easier to say yes.
- **Use incentives thoughtfully.** Gift cards and rewards can nudge action, especially when they're easy to redeem.
- **Leverage trusted messengers.** A reminder from a companion, community health worker, or familiar voice often carries more weight than a form letter.
- **Make it social.** Community events, group wellness activities, and friendly challenges can turn preventive care from a chore into something people actually look forward to. Okay, maybe not the colonoscopy. But the flu shot clinic with snacks? Absolutely.
- **Close the loop.** After a member completes a screening, follow up with results, next steps, and a thank-you.

## Small Changes, Big Results

Improving member experience doesn't always require a massive overhaul or a shiny new platform. Often, it's the small, human touches that make the biggest difference: a clearer explanation, a friendly voice, a ride to the doctor, a visit from someone who genuinely cares.

These improvements add up. Members who feel understood and supported are more satisfied, more engaged, and more likely to stay with their plan. They get the preventive care they need, manage chronic conditions more effectively, and avoid costly complications. That translates to stronger star ratings, better quality scores, and healthier plan finances.

In other words, taking care of your members is also one of the smartest ways to take care of your business. Start with one lever, measure the impact, and build from there. Your members, and your CFO, will be glad you did.
