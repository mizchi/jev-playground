# Mocha Latte Caramel Cookie Bars: All the Crunch, None of the Added Sugar

Let's be honest: some afternoons call for a candy bar. That layered, crunchy-chewy-chocolatey kind of candy bar, with a buttery cookie base, a ribbon of gooey caramel, and a snappy chocolate shell. The trouble is the sugar crash waiting on the other side of the wrapper.

So we built a better one. These homemade caramel cookie bars take everything you love about the classic and remake it with almond flour, creamy nut butter, and sugar-free dark chocolate, with Super Coffee Mocha Latte running through every layer. You get deep coffee-chocolate flavor, a bit of extra protein, and a treat that fits a low-carb lifestyle. It's dessert that pulls its weight.

## Recipe Card

**Prep time:** 25 minutes
**Cook time:** 15 minutes
**Chill time:** About 1 hour (freezer, between layers)
**Total time:** About 1 hour 40 minutes
**Yield:** 16 bars (8x8-inch pan)

### Ingredients

**Layer 1: Almond Flour Shortbread**
- 1 ½ cups blanched almond flour
- 3 tablespoons coconut flour
- 3 tablespoons granulated monk fruit or allulose sweetener
- ¼ teaspoon fine sea salt
- 4 tablespoons unsalted butter, melted
- 2 tablespoons Super Coffee Mocha Latte
- ½ teaspoon vanilla extract

**Layer 2: Nut-Butter Caramel**
- ½ cup smooth, unsweetened almond butter or cashew butter
- 3 tablespoons coconut oil, melted
- 3 tablespoons allulose syrup (or sugar-free maple-style syrup)
- 3 tablespoons Super Coffee Mocha Latte
- ½ teaspoon vanilla extract
- Pinch of flaky sea salt

**Layer 3: Sugar-Free Dark Chocolate Coating**
- 6 ounces sugar-free dark chocolate chips or chopped bar
- 1 tablespoon coconut oil
- 1 tablespoon Super Coffee Mocha Latte
- Flaky sea salt, for finishing (optional)

### Instructions

**Make the shortbread**

1. Preheat your oven to 350°F. Line an 8x8-inch baking pan with parchment paper, leaving a little overhang on two sides so you can lift the bars out later.
2. In a medium bowl, whisk together almond flour, coconut flour, sweetener, and salt.
3. Add melted butter, 2 tablespoons Mocha Latte, and vanilla. Stir until the mixture looks like damp sand and holds together when pinched.
4. Press the dough firmly and evenly into the bottom of the pan. The flat bottom of a measuring cup works well here.
5. Bake for 12 to 15 minutes, until the edges are golden. Let cool for 10 minutes on the counter, then transfer to the freezer for 15 minutes to firm up.

**Make the nut-butter caramel**

6. In a small saucepan over low heat, combine almond butter, coconut oil, allulose syrup, and 3 tablespoons Mocha Latte. Stir constantly for 2 to 3 minutes, until smooth, glossy, and slightly thickened.
7. Remove from heat and stir in vanilla and a pinch of sea salt.
8. Pour the caramel over the chilled shortbread and spread it into an even layer.
9. Return the pan to the freezer for 30 minutes, until the caramel is firm to the touch.

**Coat with chocolate**

10. In a microwave-safe bowl, combine chocolate, coconut oil, and 1 tablespoon Mocha Latte. Microwave in 20-second bursts, stirring between each, until fully melted and smooth. (Stir the coffee in slowly; the coconut oil keeps the chocolate from seizing.)
11. Pour the chocolate over the caramel layer and tilt the pan to spread it edge to edge.
12. Sprinkle lightly with flaky sea salt if you like a salty-sweet finish.
13. Freeze for 15 to 20 minutes, until the chocolate is set.
14. Lift the slab out using the parchment, then slice into 16 bars with a sharp knife warmed under hot water and wiped dry.

**Storage:** Keep bars in an airtight container in the refrigerator for up to 1 week, or in the freezer for up to 2 months. Let frozen bars sit for 5 minutes before eating for the best caramel chew.

## Why Mocha Latte Makes These Bars

Coffee and chocolate have always been best friends, and adding Mocha Latte to each layer does more than add a caffeine kick. In the shortbread, it deepens the toasty, nutty flavor of the almond flour. In the caramel, it adds a roasted, almost toffee-like richness that balances the nut butter. In the chocolate coating, it brings out the cocoa's darker notes, so every bite tastes like a café dessert rather than a health-food compromise.
