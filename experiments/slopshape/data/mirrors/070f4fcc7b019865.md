# Comfort Food, Minus the Guilt: 4 Simple Swaps That Changed How I Eat

Let me start with a confession: I used to be a junk food person. Not a "treat yourself on Fridays" person. A full-blown, drive-thru-on-the-way-home, family-size-bag-of-chips-for-one kind of person. Mac and cheese from a box was a food group. Dessert was a personality trait.

And here's my second confession: I still find healthy eating exhausting. The meal prepping. The label reading. The endless debate over whether some grain I can't pronounce is actually good for me or just trendy. I don't want to spend my Sunday chopping kale. I want food that tastes amazing and happens to be good for me, with as little effort as humanly possible.

What I've learned, slowly and with a lot of skepticism, is that you don't have to give up comfort food to eat better. You just have to swap a few things. Small changes, big difference, zero sad salads required.

Here are the four swaps that won me over, and the dishes on Eat Well's new fall menu that prove they work.

## Swap #1: Legume Pasta Instead of White Pasta

I'll be honest, I rolled my eyes the first time someone suggested chickpea pasta. Pasta is sacred. You don't mess with pasta.

But then I actually tried it, and I owe that person an apology. Pasta made from chickpeas or lentils has that same satisfying bite and soaks up sauce just like the regular stuff. The difference is what it does for you afterward. Instead of the carb crash that has me face-down on the couch an hour later, legume pasta keeps me full and steady.

**Try it in:** Our **Creamy Tuscan Chickpea Penne**, tossed in a garlicky sun-dried tomato sauce with spinach and roasted red peppers.

**Nutrition highlight:** Legume pasta packs roughly twice the protein and several times the fiber of traditional white pasta, so you stay satisfied longer without piling on extra portions.

## Swap #2: Honey Instead of Refined Sugar

I have a sweet tooth that could rival a toddler's at a birthday party. Cutting sugar entirely? Not happening. But swapping refined white sugar for honey turned out to be a surprisingly easy upgrade.

Honey brings a richer, more complex sweetness, which means you often need less of it to get that "mmm" moment. It also plays beautifully with fall flavors like cinnamon, apple, and pumpkin. Basically, it was born for autumn.

**Try it in:** Our **Honey Cinnamon Apple Crisp**, with tender baked apples and an oat crumble topping that tastes like a cozy sweater feels.

**Nutrition highlight:** Unlike refined sugar, honey contains small amounts of antioxidants and trace minerals, and its natural sweetness lets us use less added sugar overall.

## Swap #3: Almond Flour Instead of Bleached Flour

Here's a fun fact I didn't know until recently: bleached white flour is stripped of much of its natural nutrition during processing. It's basically a blank canvas, and not in a good way.

Almond flour, on the other hand, is made from finely ground almonds, so it brings actual nutrients to the party. It gives baked goods and breading a slightly nutty, golden flavor that I now genuinely prefer. And it makes things crispy. Crispy is my love language.

**Try it in:** Our **Almond-Crusted Chicken Tenders**, served with a honey mustard dip and roasted sweet potato wedges. Yes, chicken tenders. On a healthy menu. You're welcome.

**Nutrition highlight:** Almond flour is naturally gluten-free and rich in healthy fats, vitamin E, and magnesium, with far fewer carbs than bleached flour.

## Swap #4: Plant-Based Ingredients Instead of Dairy

Cheese and I have a long, committed relationship, so this one scared me the most. But plant-based ingredients have come a long way from the rubbery "cheese" of years past.

Cashew-based sauces, coconut cream, and nutritional yeast can create that creamy, rich, cheesy texture we all crave, without the heaviness that dairy sometimes brings. My stomach, for one, has sent a thank-you note.

**Try it in:** Our **Butternut Squash Mac and "Cheese,"** made with a velvety cashew and roasted squash sauce, topped with toasted breadcrumbs. It's the grown-up version of my childhood favorite, and I'm not even a little bit mad about it.

**Nutrition highlight:** Plant-based sauces like this one are lower in saturated fat and cholesterol-free, while butternut squash adds a hefty dose of vitamin A and fiber.

## The Best Part? You Don't Have to Do Any of This Yourself

Eating well shouldn't feel like a punishment or a part-time job. It should feel like pulling a delicious, comforting meal out of the fridge, heating it up, and enjoying every bite, knowing it's actually doing something good for you.
