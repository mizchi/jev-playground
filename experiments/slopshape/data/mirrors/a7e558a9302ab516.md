# Turn It Up: Our International Women's Day Playlist

There's a particular kind of magic in the women who shape us. It's in the mother who taught us to stand tall, the grandmother whose stories we still repeat, the daughter who reminds us what fearless looks like, and the friend who answers the phone at midnight, no questions asked. Sisterhood isn't just a word. It's a thread that runs through generations, stitching us together in laughter, heartbreak, and everything in between.

International Women's Day is a beautiful reminder to celebrate that thread. But honestly? We think womanhood deserves honoring every single day: in the way we lift each other up, in the way we speak kindly to ourselves, and in the small moments when we choose to take up space.

This year's theme, **#BalanceforBetter**, calls for a more gender-balanced world, in boardrooms, in government, in the media, and yes, in music. The music industry remains overwhelmingly male-dominated. A major study from the USC Annenberg Inclusion Initiative found that across hundreds of popular songs from recent years, only around one in five artists were women, and women made up a tiny fraction of producers. The numbers behind the scenes are even starker.

So we're doing our small part to tip the scales. We've put together a playlist of songs by incredible women artists, anthems that have carried women through breakups, breakthroughs, and everything in between. We've arranged it chronologically, so you can travel from 1960s soul and country all the way to the pop powerhouses of the 2010s.

Pour yourself something delicious, slip into something that feels wonderful, and press play.

## The 1960s: Finding Our Voice

**Lesley Gore – "You Don't Own Me" (1963)**
Long before it was common to hear a young woman declare her independence on the radio, Lesley Gore did it with poise and a whole lot of nerve.
*Standout lyric:* "You don't own me, don't try to change me in any way."

**Nancy Sinatra – "These Boots Are Made for Walkin'" (1966)**
Sassy, stompy, and utterly unforgettable. This one is for every woman who knows her worth and isn't afraid to walk away.
*Standout lyric:* "These boots are made for walkin', and that's just what they'll do."

**Aretha Franklin – "Respect" (1967)**
The Queen of Soul took this song and made it an anthem for women and for the civil rights movement. It still gives us chills.
*Standout lyric:* "R-E-S-P-E-C-T, find out what it means to me."

**Loretta Lynn – "Don't Come Home A-Drinkin' (With Lovin' on Your Mind)" (1966)**
The coal miner's daughter never minced words. Loretta sang plainly about the lives of real women, and country radio had to listen.
*Standout lyric:* "Don't come home a-drinkin' with lovin' on your mind."

**Dolly Parton – "Just Because I'm a Woman" (1968)**
Early in her career, Dolly was already pushing back on double standards with grace and grit. She'll appear again on this list, because of course she will.
*Standout lyric:* "Just because I'm a woman."

## The 1970s: Hear Us Roar

**Helen Reddy – "I Am Woman" (1972)**
Practically the soundtrack of the women's movement, this song is pure, unapologetic strength.
*Standout lyric:* "I am woman, hear me roar."

**Gloria Gaynor – "I Will Survive" (1978)**
Is there a more perfect song for a girls' night in? It's resilience you can dance to.
*Standout lyric:* "Oh no, not I, I will survive."

## The 1980s: Doing It for Ourselves

**Dolly Parton – "9 to 5" (1980)**
Dolly again! This time she gave working women everywhere a clever, catchy anthem about ambition and being underestimated. Her typewriter-inspired rhythm is iconic.
*Standout lyric:* "Workin' 9 to 5, what a way to make a livin'."

**Cyndi Lauper – "Girls Just Want to Have Fun" (1983)**
Joy is a radical act, and Cyndi knew it. This one is a celebration of freedom, friendship, and letting loose.
*Standout lyric:* "Oh, girls just want to have fun."

**Eurythmics & Aretha Franklin – "Sisters Are Doin' It for Themselves" (1985)**
Annie Lennox and Aretha together? It doesn't get more powerful. A true sisterhood anthem.
*Standout lyric:* "Sisters are doin' it for themselves."

## The 1990s: Just a Girl (and So Much More)

**No Doubt – "Just a Girl" (1995)**
Gwen Stefani's sharp, sarcastic take on the expectations placed on young women still feels fresh today.
*Standout lyric:* "'Cause I'm just a girl, oh, little old me."

**Shania Twain – "Man! I Feel Like a Woman!" (1997)**
Turn it up, sing along, and don't apologize for having a little fun. Pure confidence in a song.
*Standout lyric:* "Man! I feel like a woman!"

**Spice Girls – "Wannabe" (1996)**
Girl power went global with this one. It's a playful reminder that the right partner has to love your friends, too.
*Standout lyric:* "If you wanna be my lover, you have got to get with my friends."

## The 2000s: Survivors and Fighters

**Destiny's Child – "Survivor" (2001)**
Every woman has been through something, and this song reminds us we come out the other side stronger.
*Standout lyric:* "I'm a survivor, I'm not gon' give up."

**Christina Aguilera – "Beautiful" (2002)**
A tender, soaring reminder to be gentle with ourselves. We love sharing this one with the young women in our lives.
*Standout lyric:* "I am beautiful no matter what they say."

## The 2010s: Run the World

**Beyoncé – "Run the World (Girls)" (2011)**
Bold, fierce, and impossible to sit still through. Queen Bey delivers a rallying cry for women everywhere.
*Standout lyric:* "Who run the world? Girls!"

**Alicia Keys – "Girl on Fire" (2012)**
This one is for the woman who's just getting started and the one who's been blazing trails for decades.
*Standout lyric:* "This girl is on fire."

**Katy Perry – "Roar" (2013)**
A feel-good anthem about finding your voice after staying quiet for too long.
*Standout lyric:* "You're gonna hear me roar."

**Rachel Platten – "Fight Song" (2015)**
Sometimes strength starts as a whisper. This song is a reminder that small voices can make big waves.
*Standout lyric:* "This is my fight song."

## Keep the Music Playing

However you celebrate International Women's Day, whether you're gathering with your girlfriends, calling your mom, or treating yourself to a quiet evening at home, we hope these songs bring a little extra joy and power to your day.

And tomorrow, and the day after that, keep celebrating. Share this playlist with the women who inspire you. Add your own favorites. Dance around your kitchen. Sing too loud in the car.

Most of all, turn the music up and feel your strength. You've earned it.

Happy International Women's Day from all of us at Julianna Rae.
