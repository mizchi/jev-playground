# Choosing the Right Mobile Devices for Your Hospital's Clinical Mobility Initiative

Clinical mobility has moved from a nice-to-have to a core part of how hospitals deliver care. When nurses, physicians, pharmacists, and support staff can communicate, access patient information, and complete tasks at the point of care, workflows become faster, errors drop, and clinicians spend more time with patients.

But the success of any clinical mobility initiative depends heavily on one decision: the devices you put in your staff's hands. Choose well, and you get a platform that supports care for years. Choose poorly, and you face frustrated users, security gaps, and costly replacements.

Drawing on Zebra Technologies' guidance for healthcare device selection, here are the questions hospital administrators should be asking.

## Which Device Class Is Best Suited for Clinical Settings?

Hospitals have several options, from consumer smartphones to tablets to purpose-built enterprise mobile computers. For most clinical workflows, enterprise-grade healthcare mobile computers are the best fit.

Devices like Zebra's healthcare mobile computers are designed specifically for clinical environments. They combine the familiar touchscreen experience of a smartphone with features clinicians need, including integrated barcode scanning for medication administration and specimen collection, push-to-talk and voice calling, and the durability to handle drops and constant cleaning.

Tablets can be valuable for specific tasks, such as reviewing imaging or patient education, but for staff who need a device throughout their shift, a pocketable, healthcare-ready mobile computer usually delivers the most value.

## What Selection Criteria Matter Most?

Every hospital has unique needs, but a few criteria should be at the top of every evaluation.

### Security for Privacy Compliance

Mobile devices in healthcare handle protected health information, making security essential for HIPAA compliance. Look for devices that offer:

- Regular, long-term security updates and patches
- Support for encryption and secure authentication
- Integration with mobile device management (MDM) platforms
- The ability to lock down devices to approved apps and settings

Zebra devices, for example, include security enhancements and extended support for operating system updates, which helps hospitals stay protected over the life of the device.

### Disinfectant-Resistant Sealing

Infection prevention is non-negotiable. Devices must withstand frequent cleaning with the hospital-grade disinfectants your facility uses. Consumer devices often degrade under this routine, with cracked housings, damaged screens, and failing ports.

Healthcare-specific devices are built with disinfectant-ready housings and sealed designs that resist moisture and chemicals. Always confirm that a device is compatible with your approved cleaning agents.

### Voice Features

Communication is at the heart of clinical mobility. Look for devices with strong voice capabilities, including clear audio quality, push-to-talk, integration with nurse call and alarm systems, and support for your hospital's communication platform. The ability to receive critical alerts and call a colleague directly from the same device can dramatically improve response times.

## How Should You Map Current Communication and Legacy Tools?

Before choosing hardware, take a hard look at how your staff communicates and works today. Zebra recommends that hospitals map existing workflows to understand what devices will need to support.

Ask questions like:

- What devices are staff carrying now? Pagers, overhead paging, desk phones, legacy wireless phones, personal smartphones?
- How are alerts and alarms delivered?
- Which clinical applications do staff use, and where?
- Where are communication breakdowns or delays happening?

This exercise helps you identify which tools a new device can consolidate and what integrations are required. It also brings clinical stakeholders into the process early, which improves adoption.

## Why Do Enterprise Devices Beat Consumer Phones and BYOD?

Consumer smartphones and bring-your-own-device (BYOD) programs can look attractive because of lower upfront costs. However, when you consider total cost of ownership (TCO), enterprise devices typically come out ahead.

Consider the hidden costs of consumer devices and BYOD:

- **Shorter lifecycles:** Consumer phones are refreshed often, and models change frequently, making standardization difficult.
- **Durability issues:** Drops and harsh disinfectants lead to more repairs and replacements.
- **Security risks:** Personal devices are harder to secure and manage, increasing compliance risk.
- **Support burden:** A mix of devices and operating systems increases IT workload.
- **Missing features:** Consumer phones lack integrated enterprise-grade scanning and other healthcare-specific capabilities.

Enterprise devices are designed for longer service lives, stable product availability, extended OS support, and rugged construction. Over several years, those advantages translate into lower overall costs and fewer disruptions.

## Planning a Phased Rollout and Device Management

Even the best device selection needs a thoughtful deployment plan. A phased rollout, starting with a pilot unit, lets you validate workflows, gather feedback, and adjust before scaling across the hospital.

Device management is equally important. An MDM solution allows IT teams to configure, update, secure, and monitor devices remotely. Consider how devices will be charged, stored, assigned, and tracked between shifts, and plan for ongoing support and training.
