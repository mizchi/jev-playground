# 5 Ways Brands Can Use Influencers to Build Awareness (When Ads Just Aren't Cutting It)

Think about the last time you scrolled through your social feed. How many ads did you actually notice? How many did you skip, scroll past, or block entirely?

If you're like most people, the answer is "not many." And that's a real problem for brands trying to get noticed. Ad blockers are now standard on millions of browsers. Social feeds are packed with content from friends, creators, news outlets, and yes, dozens of other brands all competing for the same few seconds of attention. Traditional online advertising still has its place, but on its own it often struggles to break through.

So how do you get your brand in front of the right people in a way they'll actually pay attention to?

Enter influencer marketing.

## What Is Influencer Marketing?

At its simplest, influencer marketing is a partnership between a brand and a content creator who has a dedicated, engaged following. The creator introduces the brand's product or service to their audience, usually through content that feels natural to their feed: a video, a photo, a story, a review, or a live stream.

Instead of interrupting people with an ad, you're showing up in a space they've chosen to visit, recommended by someone they already follow and trust. That's a fundamentally different experience for the viewer, and it's a big reason influencer marketing has grown from a niche tactic into a core part of modern marketing strategies.

## The Case for Micro- and Nano-Influencers

When people hear "influencer," they often picture a celebrity with millions of followers. And while those big names can deliver massive reach, they're not always the best fit, especially for brands focused on building genuine awareness and trust.

That's where micro-influencers (typically around 10,000 to 100,000 followers) and nano-influencers (roughly 1,000 to 10,000 followers) come in.

Here's why they're so powerful:

- **They're relatable.** Smaller creators often feel like a friend rather than a far-off star. Their recommendations come across as authentic because their lives look a lot like their followers' lives.
- **They drive higher engagement.** Engagement rates tend to be stronger among smaller creators. Their followers comment, ask questions, and actually interact, which means your brand gets real conversation instead of passive impressions.
- **They have niche, loyal audiences.** A nano-influencer who posts about vegan baking or trail running has an audience that cares deeply about that topic. If your product fits, you're reaching exactly the right people.
- **They're budget-friendly.** Working with smaller creators is generally more affordable, making it possible to partner with several creators at once and multiply your reach.

For businesses at any stage, from scrappy startups to established brands, micro- and nano-influencers offer an accessible and effective way to get started.

Now let's dig into five ways you can put influencers to work building awareness for your brand.

## 1. Hand Over the Content Creation

One of the smartest moves a brand can make is to let influencers do what they do best: create.

Influencers know their audience inside and out. They know which formats perform, what tone resonates, and what makes their followers stop scrolling. When you give them creative freedom (within clear brand guidelines), the content they produce feels native to their feed rather than like a polished ad dropped in from outside.

The benefits go beyond a single post, too. Influencer-created content can often be repurposed across your own channels (with permission and proper usage rights), including your website, social accounts, email campaigns, and even paid ads. That gives you a steady stream of fresh, authentic content without the cost and time of producing it all in-house.

**Tip:** Provide a creative brief that covers your key messages, must-include details, and anything that's off-limits, but leave room for the creator's personality to shine through. The best results usually come from collaboration, not a script.

## 2. Unboxing and Tutorial Videos

There's something oddly satisfying about watching someone open a package. Unboxing videos have become a favorite format because they let viewers experience a product's first impression right alongside the creator: the packaging, the reveal, the initial reaction.

Tutorial videos take it a step further. They show your product in action and help viewers understand exactly how it works and why it's useful. For products that need a little explaining, such as a skincare routine, a kitchen gadget, a piece of software, or a fitness tool, a tutorial can do more to build understanding and interest than any static ad.

Both formats work because they answer the questions shoppers naturally have:

- What does it look like in real life?
- How does it work?
- Is it actually worth it?

When a trusted creator answers those questions honestly and enthusiastically, viewers walk away not just aware of your brand but genuinely curious about it.

## 3. Social Media Takeovers

A social media takeover is exactly what it sounds like: an influencer temporarily takes control of your brand's social account, usually for a day or a set number of posts or stories.

This tactic is a two-way awareness booster. The influencer promotes the takeover to their own followers, driving new eyes to your account. Meanwhile, your existing followers get a fresh perspective and a new personality on your feed, which can spark renewed engagement.

Takeovers work especially well for:

- Behind-the-scenes looks at your brand, team, or product development.
- "A day in the life" content showing how your product fits into everyday routines.
- Live Q&A sessions where followers can ask the creator questions directly.
- Event coverage, product launches, or special announcements.

**Tip:** Plan the takeover together ahead of time so both sides know what content will go live and when. Set clear guidelines, but let the influencer's voice lead. That's the whole point.

## 4. Giveaway Contests with Reach-Boosting Rules

Everybody loves free stuff. Giveaways are one of the fastest ways to generate buzz, and when you team up with an influencer, the effect can be amplified significantly.

The secret is in the entry rules. By structuring your giveaway thoughtfully, you can encourage participants to spread the word for you. Common reach-boosting rules include:

- **Follow both accounts.** Participants must follow your brand and the influencer to enter.
- **Tag friends in the comments.** Each tag introduces your brand to someone new, and many platforms let you offer additional entries for additional tags.
- **Share the post to stories.** This puts the giveaway in front of each participant's own network.
- **Create user-generated content.** For a bigger commitment, ask participants to post their own photo or video using a branded hashtag.

Each of these actions turns participants into mini-ambassadors, pushing your brand's visibility far beyond the influencer's initial audience.

**Tip:** Make sure your giveaway complies with each platform's promotion guidelines and any applicable local laws. Keep the rules simple and clear, too. If entering feels like homework, people won't bother.

## 5. Brand Events Featuring Influencers

Whether it's a product launch, a pop-up shop, a workshop, or a virtual event, inviting influencers to attend or even co-host can transform a local gathering into a far-reaching moment.

Influencers bring two big advantages to brand events. First, their presence can draw attendees, since fans love the chance to meet creators they follow. Second, they document the experience in real time, sharing photos, videos, and stories with their audience. Suddenly, an event with a hundred people in the room has been seen by tens of thousands more online.

Virtual events deserve a mention here too. Live streams, webinars, and online workshops featuring influencers can reach audiences anywhere in the world, with no venue required. They're a fantastic option for brands with limited budgets or a geographically spread-out customer base.

**Tip:** Create "shareable moments" at your events: eye-catching displays, interactive experiences, exclusive previews. Give influencers something worth posting about, and they'll do the rest.

## Why Influencer Marketing Works

So what makes influencer marketing so effective at cutting through the noise? It comes down to three things.

### Social Proof

People look to others when deciding what to buy. When someone we follow and admire uses and recommends a product, it signals that the product is worth our attention. That's social proof in action, and it's one of the most powerful forces in consumer behavior.

### Receptive Audiences

Followers choose to follow influencers. They're actively seeking out their content, which means they're in a receptive mindset when they see it. Compare that to a banner ad that interrupts what someone's reading, and it's easy to see why influencer content tends to land better.

### Trust

Above all, influencer marketing is built on trust. Creators build relationships with their audiences over months and years by sharing honest opinions, personal stories, and helpful advice. When they recommend a brand, some of that trust transfers to the brand. That's something traditional advertising simply can't buy.

## Getting Started: Choosing Partners, Compensation, and KPIs

Ready to jump in? Here's some practical advice to help you set up your influencer campaigns for success.

### Choose Well-Matched Partners

Follower count isn't everything. The right influencer is one whose audience matches your target customers and whose values align with your brand. When evaluating potential partners, look at:

- **Audience demographics:** Age, location, interests, and whether they align with your ideal customer.
- **Engagement quality:** Are followers genuinely interacting, or are comments generic and sparse?
- **Content style and tone:** Does their aesthetic and voice fit your brand?
- **Authenticity:** Do they promote products they genuinely use, or does their feed feel like a string of ads?

A well-matched micro-influencer will almost always outperform a mismatched mega-influencer.

### Agree on Compensation

Be clear and upfront about compensation from the start. Depending on the creator and the campaign, compensation might include:

- A flat fee per post or per campaign.
- Free products or services.
- Commission or affiliate earnings based on sales.
- A combination of the above.

Put everything in writing, including deliverables, timelines, usage rights, and disclosure requirements. Creators should always clearly disclose sponsored content in line with advertising regulations, and a good partnership agreement makes that expectation explicit.

### Set KPIs to Measure Results

Finally, decide how you'll measure success before the campaign launches. For awareness-focused campaigns, useful KPIs include:

- **Reach and impressions:** How many people saw the content.
- **Engagement:** Likes, comments, shares, and saves.
- **Follower growth:** New followers gained on your brand accounts.
- **Website traffic:** Visits driven by tracked links or promo codes.
- **Branded search and mentions:** Increases in people searching for or talking about your brand.

Tracking these metrics helps you understand what's working, which creators deliver the best results, and where to invest next.

## The Bottom Line

Ad blockers and crowded feeds have made it harder than ever to get noticed through traditional online advertising. But influencer marketing offers a different path, one built on authenticity, community, and trust.

Whether you hand over content creation, spark curiosity with unboxing and tutorials, shake things up with a takeover, fuel buzz with a giveaway, or bring your brand to life at an event, influencers can help you reach people in a way that feels genuine rather than intrusive.

And the best part? You don't need a massive budget or a celebrity endorsement to make it work. With the right micro- and nano-influencer partners, clear agreements, and thoughtful goals, brands of any size can build awareness that sticks.
