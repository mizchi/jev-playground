# Staying Close from Afar: 4 Ways Our Leads Keep Remote Teams Engaged and Connected

Ask almost anyone what they love about remote work, and you'll hear the same things: no commute, more flexibility, time with family, and the freedom to work from wherever feels most like home. It's no surprise that remote work has become one of the most prized perks a company can offer.

But anyone who has worked remotely for long knows there's another side to the story. Without the hallway chats, shared lunches, and quick "hey, got a minute?" moments, it's easy for people to start feeling like they're working alone. Isolation creeps in. Engagement dips. Morale quietly slides, often without anyone noticing until someone decides to leave.

At Boldr, our teams are spread across countries and time zones, so we think about this a lot. Connection doesn't happen by accident when people aren't sharing an office. It takes intention, consistency, and a genuine care for the people behind the screens.

We asked a few of our department leads how they keep their teams engaged and connected. Here's what they shared.

## 1. A Weekly Wrap-Up Ritual

The end of the week can easily blur into the start of the next one when you work remotely. There's no walk out the office door, no Friday afternoon wind-down with colleagues. One of our leads decided to create that moment on purpose.

"Every Friday, we close the week together," our Operations Lead explains. "It's a short call, nothing formal. Everyone shares one win from the week, one thing that was hard, and one thing they're looking forward to. Sometimes the 'win' is closing a big project. Sometimes it's just finally fixing their Wi-Fi. Both count."

What started as a simple habit has become something the team genuinely looks forward to. "It gives people a chance to be seen," she says. "In a remote setting, a lot of good work happens quietly. The wrap-up is where we celebrate it. And when someone's had a rough week, it's where the rest of the team can rally around them."

The ritual also creates a sense of closure. "People log off on Friday feeling like the week had a beginning, a middle, and an end. That matters more than you'd think."

**Try it:** Keep it short, keep it consistent, and let people share both highs and lows. The power is in the routine.

## 2. Regular Personal Check-Ins That Normalize Talking About Wellbeing

Status updates tell you how the work is going. They don't always tell you how the *person* is doing. That's why one of our leads makes personal check-ins a non-negotiable part of how she manages.

"I schedule one-on-ones with everyone on my team, and I try to spend the first part of every conversation not talking about work at all," says our People and Culture Lead. "I'll ask how they're sleeping, how their family is, whether they're getting any time for themselves. At first, people were surprised. Now they bring it up before I even ask."

For her, the goal is to make wellbeing a normal topic, not something that only comes up when there's a crisis. "When you work remotely, you can't see when someone's struggling. There's no slumped shoulders at the next desk. So you have to create space for people to tell you. And they'll only tell you if they believe it's safe and that you actually care."

She's seen the difference it makes firsthand. "One teammate told me during a check-in that she was burning out because she'd been answering messages late into the night. We talked it through, adjusted her workload, and set clearer boundaries for the team. If we hadn't had that conversation, I'm not sure she would have stayed."

**Try it:** Put personal check-ins on the calendar and protect them. Ask real questions, listen to the answers, and follow through when someone needs support.

## 3. Food-Centered Bonding Rooted in Filipino Culture

Many of our team members are based in the Philippines, where food is at the heart of connection. Sharing a meal is how people celebrate, comfort each other, and simply spend time together. One of our leads found a way to bring that tradition into remote work.

"In Filipino culture, one of the first things people ask each other is, '*Kumain ka na?*' Have you eaten?" says our Client Services Lead. "It's not really about food. It's a way of saying 'I care about you.' So we built that into how our team connects."

Her team holds regular virtual meals where everyone eats together on camera. Sometimes they all order the same dish. Sometimes they each share a favorite from home and talk about the memories behind it. For birthdays and milestones, the team sends food deliveries so the celebration feels real, even from a distance.

"We've had *merienda* breaks where people show off their snacks, and we've done cooking sessions where someone teaches the rest of us a family recipe," she says. "Our teammates in other countries love it. They've learned about *adobo*, *pancit*, and *halo-halo*, and they've shared dishes from their own cultures too. It turns into this beautiful exchange."

For her, it's about honoring where people come from. "When you bring your culture into how you connect, people feel like they can bring their whole selves to work. That's when real bonds form."

**Try it:** Look at the traditions your team already values and find ways to bring them into your remote routines. Shared meals are a wonderful place to start.

## 4. Monthly Playful Icebreaker Sessions for a Team Spread Across Countries

When your team spans multiple countries, getting to know each other can be tricky. Different time zones, languages, and cultures can make it feel like colleagues are strangers who happen to share a Slack channel. One of our leads tackles that head-on with a little fun.

"Once a month, we get everyone together for an icebreaker session," says our Technology Lead. "No agenda, no work talk. We play games, answer silly questions, and just laugh together."

The activities change every month. "We've done 'two truths and a lie,' virtual scavenger hunts where people have to find something in their house in 60 seconds, and 'show and tell' where everyone shares something meaningful from their home. One month, we had everyone share a photo of their view from the window. We saw city skylines, rice fields, snowy streets. It really drove home how spread out we are, and how lucky we are to work together."

He's careful to rotate meeting times so no one region always gets stuck with a late-night or early-morning slot. "It's a small thing, but it shows people that everyone's time matters equally."

The payoff has been huge. "People who'd never spoken before now message each other outside of work. When you've laughed with someone, it's so much easier to ask them for help or give them feedback. The collaboration just flows better."

**Try it:** Keep it light, keep it inclusive, and rotate schedules so everyone can join. Fun is a surprisingly serious tool for building a team.

## Why Connection Matters: Trust, Retention, and Productivity

These tactics might seem small, but their impact is anything but. When remote team members feel connected, good things happen across the board.

**Trust grows.** People who know each other beyond job titles are more willing to share ideas, ask for help, and give honest feedback. Trust is the foundation of every high-performing team, and connection is how it's built.

**Retention improves.** Employees rarely leave companies where they feel valued and seen. When people have real relationships with their colleagues and managers, they're far more likely to stay, even when other opportunities come knocking.

**Productivity rises.** Engaged employees bring more energy, creativity, and care to their work. Teams that communicate openly solve problems faster and collaborate more smoothly.

Put simply, investing in connection isn't just the kind thing to do. It's the smart thing to do.
