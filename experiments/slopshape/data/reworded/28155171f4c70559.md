# 5 Ways Brands Can Use Influencers to Build Awareness (When Ads Just Aren't Cutting It)

Recall the last time you scrolled your social feed. How many ads did you notice? How many did you skip, scroll past, or block?

For most people, the answer is "not many," and that is a real problem for brands that want to be seen. Millions of browsers now run ad blockers by default. Social feeds are crowded with posts from friends, creators, news outlets, and dozens of other brands, all fighting for the same few seconds of attention. Traditional online advertising still has a role, but by itself it often fails to get through.

How, then, do you reach the right people in a way they will actually notice?

Influencer marketing is one answer.

## What Is Influencer Marketing?

Put simply, influencer marketing is a partnership between a brand and a content creator with a dedicated, engaged following. The creator presents the brand's product or service to their audience, usually in a format that already fits their feed: a video, a photo, a story, a review, or a live stream.

Rather than interrupting people with an ad, the brand appears in a space they chose to visit, recommended by someone they already follow and trust. For the viewer, that is a very different experience, and it largely explains why influencer marketing has moved from a niche tactic to a core part of modern marketing strategies.

## The Case for Micro- and Nano-Influencers

The word "influencer" often brings to mind a celebrity with millions of followers. Those big names can deliver enormous reach, but they aren't always the right choice, particularly for brands trying to build genuine awareness and trust.

Micro-influencers (typically around 10,000 to 100,000 followers) and nano-influencers (roughly 1,000 to 10,000 followers) fill that gap.

Their strengths:

- **They're relatable.** Smaller creators tend to seem like friends rather than distant stars. Because their lives resemble their followers' lives, their recommendations read as authentic.
- **They drive higher engagement.** Smaller creators usually see stronger engagement rates. Their followers comment, ask questions, and interact, so your brand gets real conversation instead of passive impressions.
- **They have niche, loyal audiences.** A nano-influencer who posts about vegan baking or trail running speaks to people who care a great deal about that subject. If your product fits, you reach exactly the right buyers.
- **They're budget-friendly.** Smaller creators generally cost less, so you can partner with several at once and multiply your reach.

Whether you run a scrappy startup or an established brand, micro- and nano-influencers are an accessible and effective place to begin.

Here are five ways to use influencers to build awareness for your brand.

## 1. Hand Over the Content Creation

One of the best decisions a brand can make is to let influencers handle what they are good at: creating content.

Influencers know their audience well. They know which formats perform, which tone lands, and what makes their followers stop scrolling. Given creative freedom within clear brand guidelines, they produce content that fits their feed instead of looking like a polished ad inserted from outside.

The value extends past one post. With permission and proper usage rights, influencer-created content can often be reused on your own channels, including your website, social accounts, email campaigns, and even paid ads. That supplies a steady flow of fresh, authentic content without the cost and time of making it all in-house.

**Tip:** Write a creative brief that lists your key messages, required details, and anything off-limits, but leave space for the creator's personality. Collaboration usually beats a script.

## 2. Unboxing and Tutorial Videos

Watching someone open a package is oddly satisfying. Unboxing videos have become a popular format because viewers share the creator's first impression of a product: the packaging, the reveal, the initial reaction.

Tutorial videos go further. They show your product in use and help viewers see exactly how it works and why it's useful. For products that need some explanation, such as a skincare routine, a kitchen gadget, a piece of software, or a fitness tool, a tutorial can build understanding and interest better than any static ad.

Both formats succeed because they answer the questions shoppers already have:

- What does it look like in real life?
- How does it work?
- Is it actually worth it?

When a trusted creator answers those questions honestly and with enthusiasm, viewers leave both aware of your brand and curious about it.

## 3. Social Media Takeovers

In a social media takeover, an influencer runs your brand's social account for a limited time, usually a day or a set number of posts or stories.

The tactic raises awareness in two directions. The influencer promotes the takeover to their own followers, sending new visitors to your account. Your existing followers, meanwhile, see a new perspective and personality on your feed, which can revive engagement.

Takeovers are especially effective for:

- Behind-the-scenes looks at your brand, team, or product development.
- "A day in the life" content showing how your product fits into everyday routines.
- Live Q&A sessions where followers can ask the creator questions directly.
- Event coverage, product launches, or special announcements.

**Tip:** Plan the takeover together in advance so both sides know what content will go live and when. Set clear guidelines, but let the influencer's voice lead, since that is the reason for doing a takeover.

## 4. Giveaway Contests with Reach-Boosting Rules

People love free things. Giveaways generate buzz quickly, and partnering with an influencer can amplify that effect considerably.

The entry rules make the difference. A well-structured giveaway gets participants to spread the word for you. Common reach-boosting rules include:

- **Follow both accounts.** Entrants must follow your brand and the influencer.
- **Tag friends in the comments.** Each tag introduces your brand to someone new, and many platforms let you award extra entries for extra tags.
- **Share the post to stories.** This shows the giveaway to each participant's own network.
- **Create user-generated content.** For a bigger commitment, ask participants to post their own photo or video with a branded hashtag.

Each action makes participants into small-scale ambassadors, extending your brand's visibility well past the influencer's original audience.

**Tip:** Check that your giveaway follows each platform's promotion guidelines and any applicable local laws. Keep the rules simple and clear as well; if entering feels like homework, people will skip it.

## 5. Brand Events Featuring Influencers

For a product launch, a pop-up shop, a workshop, or a virtual event, inviting influencers to attend or co-host can turn a local gathering into something seen far beyond the venue.

Influencers add two major benefits to brand events. First, they can attract attendees, because fans welcome the chance to meet creators they follow. Second, they document the event as it happens, posting photos, videos, and stories for their audience. An event with a hundred people in the room ends up seen by tens of thousands more online.

Virtual events also deserve attention. Live streams, webinars, and online workshops featuring influencers can reach audiences anywhere in the world without a venue. They suit brands with limited budgets or customers spread across a wide area.

**Tip:** Build "shareable moments" into your events, such as eye-catching displays, interactive experiences, and exclusive previews. If influencers have something worth posting about, they will handle the rest.

## Why Influencer Marketing Works

Why is influencer marketing so good at getting past the noise? Three factors explain it.

### Social Proof

People take cues from others when deciding what to buy. When someone we follow and admire uses and recommends a product, that suggests the product deserves our attention. This is social proof, one of the strongest forces in consumer behavior.

### Receptive Audiences

Followers choose to follow influencers. Because they seek out that content, they are in a receptive frame of mind when they see it. A banner ad, by contrast, interrupts whatever someone is reading, which is why influencer content usually lands better.

### Trust

Most of all, influencer marketing depends on trust. Creators develop relationships with their audiences over months and years by sharing honest opinions, personal stories, and useful advice. When they recommend a brand, part of that trust carries over to it, and traditional advertising cannot buy that.

## Getting Started: Choosing Partners, Compensation, and KPIs

If you're ready to begin, the following advice will help you set up influencer campaigns that succeed.

### Choose Well-Matched Partners

Follower count is only one factor. The right influencer has an audience that matches your target customers and values that fit your brand. When assessing potential partners, examine:

- **Audience demographics:** Age, location, interests, and how closely they match your ideal customer.
- **Engagement quality:** Are followers genuinely interacting, or are comments generic and sparse?
- **Content style and tone:** Do their aesthetic and voice suit your brand?
- **Authenticity:** Do they promote products they genuinely use, or does their feed read like a string of ads?

A well-matched micro-influencer will almost always outperform a mismatched mega-influencer.

### Agree on Compensation

Settle compensation clearly at the outset. Depending on the creator and the campaign, it might include:

- A flat fee per post or per campaign.
- Free products or services.
- Commission or affiliate earnings based on sales.
- A combination of the above.

Document everything in writing, including deliverables, timelines, usage rights, and disclosure requirements. Creators should always clearly disclose sponsored content in line with advertising regulations, and a good partnership agreement states that expectation explicitly.

### Set KPIs to Measure Results

Last, define how you will measure success before the campaign goes live. For awareness-focused campaigns, useful KPIs include:

- **Reach and impressions:** How many people saw the content.
- **Engagement:** Likes, comments, shares, and saves.
- **Follower growth:** New followers gained on your brand accounts.
- **Website traffic:** Visits driven by tracked links or promo codes.
- **Branded search and mentions:** Increases in people searching for or talking about your brand.

These metrics show you what's working, which creators produce the best results, and where to invest next.

## The Bottom Line

Ad blockers and crowded feeds have made it harder than ever to stand out with traditional online advertising. Influencer marketing takes a different route, grounded in authenticity, community, and trust.

Handing over content creation, sparking curiosity with unboxings and tutorials, refreshing your feed with a takeover, generating buzz with a giveaway, or bringing your brand to life at an event can all help you reach people in a way that feels genuine instead of intrusive.

You also don't need a huge budget or a celebrity endorsement. With well-chosen micro- and nano-influencer partners, clear agreements, and thoughtful goals, brands of any size can build lasting awareness.
