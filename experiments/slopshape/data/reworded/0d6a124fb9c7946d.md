# How a Diesel Fuel Card Takes the Busywork Out of Running a Trucking Operation

If you're an owner-operator driving your own rig or you manage a fleet of hundreds of trucks, you know fuel ranks among your largest expenses. What people often miss is the time it takes to manage. Gathering receipts, reconciling statements, watching for unauthorized purchases, working out fuel taxes state by state, and preparing reports for your accountant can take hours each week.

A diesel fuel card is built to take over much of that work. A good program does more than save you money at the pump. It gives you tighter control over spending, cleaner records, and simpler tax compliance, which means less time on paperwork and more time keeping your trucks on the road.

Below is how a fuel card can simplify your operation and what to weigh when you pick one.

## Fuel Cards Work for Operations of Every Size

Many people assume fuel cards are only for large fleets. In fact, nearly any trucking business can benefit from one.

**Owner-operators** get an easy way to keep business fuel separate from personal spending, track every transaction, and produce the records they need at tax time instead of sorting through a glove box stuffed with receipts.

**Small and mid-sized fleets** can see how much each driver spends and where, and they get tools that stop misuse before it happens.

**Large fleets** can manage fuel centrally for dozens or hundreds of vehicles, apply the same policies across all of them, and pull detailed reports for budgeting and cost analysis.

Whatever your size, the aim is the same: spend less time chasing paperwork and be more confident that your fuel dollars go where you meant them to.

## Cardlock vs. Universal Cards: Understanding Your Options

Fuel cards don't all work alike. The two main kinds are cardlock programs and universal fuel cards, and each has its own strengths.

### Cardlock Programs

You use cardlock fuel cards at dedicated fueling sites, which are often unattended stations designed for commercial vehicles. Many of these programs price fuel at wholesale or cost-plus rates instead of retail pump prices, and that can add up to real savings when you buy diesel in high volume.

The downside is coverage, since cardlock networks usually have fewer locations than retail station networks. They can be an excellent fit if your trucks run regular routes near cardlock sites. If your drivers cover unpredictable territory, you may run into gaps.

### Universal Fuel Cards

Universal fuel cards work at a wide network of fuel stations, including major brands and truck stops. That flexibility suits long-haul operations and fleets that cover large regions. Because drivers can fuel almost anywhere, they drive fewer out-of-route miles and lose less time looking for an approved station.

Universal card pricing is usually based on retail prices, sometimes with per-gallon rebates or discounts.

### Look Beyond the Discount

Choosing a fuel card only by its advertised discount is tempting. A few cents off per gallon means little, though, if fees wipe it out. Before you sign up, go over the program's complete fee structure, including:

- Monthly or annual card fees
- Transaction fees
- Out-of-network fees
- Late payment fees and finance charges
- Fees for reports or additional services

Work out what you would really pay based on how you'll actually use the card. The best program gives you the lowest total cost and the features you need, which may not be the one with the largest headline discount.

## Driver Spending Controls and Alerts

A major benefit of a fuel card is that you can set rules for how it's used. Rather than assuming every purchase is legitimate, you build safeguards into the card itself.

Typical controls include:

- **Purchase type restrictions**, limiting cards to diesel only or allowing specific additional items such as DEF
- **Daily, weekly, or per-transaction limits** on gallons or dollar amounts
- **Time-of-day and day-of-week restrictions**
- **Location restrictions** to keep purchases within certain regions
- **Driver ID or PIN requirements** so every transaction is tied to a specific driver and vehicle

Alerts give you another layer of protection. You can get a notification whenever a transaction falls outside the limits you set, such as an unusually large purchase or a fill-up at an odd hour. You can then deal with a possible problem right away instead of finding it on a statement weeks later.

## Route Planning Made Easier

Many fuel card programs come with a mobile app that shows drivers in-network stations along their route. Drivers can view locations, compare fuel prices, and plan stops ahead of time, so they avoid costly off-network purchases and needless detours.

Fleet managers get fewer surprises and steadier fuel costs, and drivers do less guessing on the road.

## Automatic IFTA Tax Tracking

If your trucks cross state lines, you already know how much time International Fuel Tax Agreement reporting takes. You must track gallons bought and miles driven in each jurisdiction and then file quarterly returns.

A fuel card simplifies this by automatically recording where each gallon was bought, organized by state. You get organized data to support your IFTA filings instead of assembling receipts by hand. That cuts errors, saves hours every quarter, and leaves you with a clear record in case of an audit.

## Off-Road Fuel Tax Deductions for Dual-Purpose Equipment

Some trucks and equipment burn fuel both for on-road driving and for off-road uses, such as running a refrigeration unit, a pump, a power take-off, or other auxiliary equipment. Fuel used for qualifying off-road purposes may be eligible for tax refunds or credits.

That usage is hard to track manually. A suitable fuel card program can categorize and document fuel purchases so you can identify and support these off-road fuel tax deductions. Over time, those savings can add up to money you might otherwise have missed.

## Custom Accounting Reports

Businesses each track their finances in slightly different ways. Fuel card programs usually include reporting tools that let you view and export data in whatever format suits you.

You can run reports by driver, vehicle, department, location, date range, or fuel type. Many programs export data straight into accounting software, which reduces manual data entry and makes month-end reconciliation quicker and more accurate. Clear reports also help you notice trends, like a truck with falling fuel economy or a driver who spends much more than the others.

## California Agricultural Sales Tax Exemption

Fuel cards can also help California agricultural operations save on sales tax. California provides a partial sales and use tax exemption on diesel fuel used in farming activities and food processing. Qualifying businesses can apply it to eligible purchases, and a fuel card program that supports the exemption can help make sure it is applied and documented correctly.

If your business qualifies, the exemption can noticeably lower your fuel costs over a year.

## Less Busywork, More Oversight

Running a trucking operation is hard enough without spending your evenings sorting receipts and building spreadsheets. A diesel fuel card puts all your fuel spending in one place, lets you control how drivers use it, and makes tax tracking and reporting simpler.

What matters most is picking a program that matches how your business really runs. Think about where your trucks go, how much fuel you buy, which controls you need, and what the program costs after every fee is counted. With the right card, you'll get tighter oversight, cleaner records, and more time to grow your business.
