# Comfort Food, Minus the Guilt: 4 Simple Swaps That Changed How I Eat

First, a confession: I used to eat junk food constantly. I wasn't a "treat yourself on Fridays" eater. I hit the drive-thru on the way home and finished family-size bags of chips by myself. Boxed mac and cheese counted as a food group. Dessert was a personality trait.

Second confession: healthy eating still wears me out. There's the meal prep, the label reading, and the constant question of whether some grain I can't pronounce is actually good for me or just trendy. I have no interest in spending Sunday chopping kale. I want food that tastes amazing, happens to be good for me, and takes as little effort as possible.

Slowly, and with plenty of skepticism, I've learned that eating better doesn't mean giving up comfort food. It means swapping a few ingredients. The changes are small, the difference is big, and you don't have to eat a single sad salad.

These are the four swaps that convinced me, plus the dishes on Eat Well's new fall menu that show they work.

## Swap #1: Legume Pasta Instead of White Pasta

Honestly, when someone first suggested chickpea pasta, I rolled my eyes. Pasta is sacred, and you don't mess with it.

Then I tried it, and now I owe that person an apology. Chickpea or lentil pasta has the same satisfying bite as regular pasta and soaks up sauce just as well. Where it differs is how I feel afterward. White pasta leaves me face-down on the couch an hour later from the carb crash; legume pasta keeps me full and steady.

**Try it in:** Our **Creamy Tuscan Chickpea Penne**, tossed in a garlicky sun-dried tomato sauce with spinach and roasted red peppers.

**Nutrition highlight:** Legume pasta has roughly twice the protein and several times the fiber of traditional white pasta, so you stay full longer without reaching for extra portions.

## Swap #2: Honey Instead of Refined Sugar

My sweet tooth could compete with a toddler's at a birthday party, so giving up sugar completely isn't going to happen. Replacing refined white sugar with honey, though, was an easier upgrade than I expected.

Honey's sweetness is richer and more complex, so you often need less of it to hit that "mmm" moment. It also pairs really well with fall flavors like cinnamon, apple, and pumpkin, which makes it a natural fit for autumn.

**Try it in:** Our **Honey Cinnamon Apple Crisp**, with tender baked apples and an oat crumble topping that tastes the way a cozy sweater feels.

**Nutrition highlight:** Honey has small amounts of antioxidants and trace minerals that refined sugar lacks, and because it's naturally sweet, we can use less added sugar overall.

## Swap #3: Almond Flour Instead of Bleached Flour

Something I only learned recently: processing strips bleached white flour of much of its natural nutrition. What's left is basically a blank canvas, and not in a good way.

Almond flour is just finely ground almonds, so it contributes real nutrients. It gives baked goods and breading a slightly nutty, golden flavor that I now honestly prefer. It also makes things crispy, and crispy is my love language.

**Try it in:** Our **Almond-Crusted Chicken Tenders**, served with a honey mustard dip and roasted sweet potato wedges. Yes, chicken tenders. On a healthy menu. You're welcome.

**Nutrition highlight:** Almond flour is naturally gluten-free, has far fewer carbs than bleached flour, and is rich in healthy fats, vitamin E, and magnesium.

## Swap #4: Plant-Based Ingredients Instead of Dairy

Cheese and I have been in a long, committed relationship, so this swap scared me more than the others. Plant-based ingredients, though, are much better than the rubbery "cheese" of years ago.

Cashew-based sauces, coconut cream, and nutritional yeast can deliver the creamy, rich, cheesy texture we all want, minus the heaviness dairy sometimes brings. My stomach, for one, has sent a thank-you note.

**Try it in:** Our **Butternut Squash Mac and "Cheese,"** made with a velvety cashew and roasted squash sauce and topped with toasted breadcrumbs. It's a grown-up take on my childhood favorite, and I'm not even a little bit mad about it.

**Nutrition highlight:** Plant-based sauces like this one contain no cholesterol and less saturated fat, and the butternut squash contributes plenty of vitamin A and fiber.
