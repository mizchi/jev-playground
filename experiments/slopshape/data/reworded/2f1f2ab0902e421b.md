# Staying Close from Afar: 4 Ways Our Leads Keep Remote Teams Engaged and Connected

Ask people what they like about remote work and the answers repeat: no commute, more flexibility, time with family, and the freedom to work from whatever place feels most like home. Remote work has become one of the most valued perks a company can offer.

Anyone who has worked remotely for a while also knows the downside. Without hallway chats, shared lunches, and quick "hey, got a minute?" moments, people can start to feel they're working alone. Isolation sets in, engagement drops, and morale declines, often unnoticed until someone decides to leave.

At Boldr, our teams work across countries and time zones, so this is something we think about constantly. When people don't share an office, connection has to be built deliberately and consistently, by leaders who care about the people on the other side of the screen.

We asked several of our department leads how they keep their teams engaged and connected. Here are their answers.

## 1. A Weekly Wrap-Up Ritual

In remote work, the end of one week can run straight into the start of the next. Nobody walks out the office door or winds down with colleagues on Friday afternoon. One of our leads decided to create that moment deliberately.

"Every Friday, we close the week together," our Operations Lead explains. "It's a short call, nothing formal. Everyone shares one win from the week, one thing that was hard, and one thing they're looking forward to. Sometimes the 'win' is closing a big project. Sometimes it's just finally fixing their Wi-Fi. Both count."

The simple habit has turned into something the team looks forward to. "It gives people a chance to be seen," she says. "In a remote setting, a lot of good work happens quietly. The wrap-up is where we celebrate it. And when someone's had a rough week, it's where the rest of the team can rally around them."

The call also gives the week an ending. "People log off on Friday feeling like the week had a beginning, a middle, and an end. That matters more than you'd think."

**Try it:** Keep the call short and regular, and invite people to share both highs and lows. The value comes from the routine.

## 2. Regular Personal Check-Ins That Normalize Talking About Wellbeing

Status updates show how the work is going, but not necessarily how the *person* is doing. For that reason, one of our leads treats personal check-ins as a fixed part of how she manages.

"I schedule one-on-ones with everyone on my team, and I try to spend the first part of every conversation not talking about work at all," says our People and Culture Lead. "I'll ask how they're sleeping, how their family is, whether they're getting any time for themselves. At first, people were surprised. Now they bring it up before I even ask."

Her aim is to make wellbeing an ordinary topic instead of one that surfaces only in a crisis. "When you work remotely, you can't see when someone's struggling. There's no slumped shoulders at the next desk. So you have to create space for people to tell you. And they'll only tell you if they believe it's safe and that you actually care."

She has watched it make a difference. "One teammate told me during a check-in that she was burning out because she'd been answering messages late into the night. We talked it through, adjusted her workload, and set clearer boundaries for the team. If we hadn't had that conversation, I'm not sure she would have stayed."

**Try it:** Schedule personal check-ins and don't let other meetings displace them. Ask real questions, listen to the answers, and follow up when someone needs support.

## 3. Food-Centered Bonding Rooted in Filipino Culture

Many of our team members live in the Philippines, where food is central to how people connect. Sharing a meal is how people celebrate, comfort one another, and simply spend time together. One of our leads found a way to carry that tradition into remote work.

"In Filipino culture, one of the first things people ask each other is, '*Kumain ka na?*' Have you eaten?" says our Client Services Lead. "It's not really about food. It's a way of saying 'I care about you.' So we built that into how our team connects."

Her team holds regular virtual meals where everyone eats together on camera. Sometimes they all order the same dish; other times each person shares a favorite from home and the memories attached to it. For birthdays and milestones, the team sends food deliveries so the celebration feels real despite the distance.

"We've had *merienda* breaks where people show off their snacks, and we've done cooking sessions where someone teaches the rest of us a family recipe," she says. "Our teammates in other countries love it. They've learned about *adobo*, *pancit*, and *halo-halo*, and they've shared dishes from their own cultures too. It turns into this beautiful exchange."

She sees it as a way of respecting where people come from. "When you bring your culture into how you connect, people feel like they can bring their whole selves to work. That's when real bonds form."

**Try it:** Identify traditions your team already values and work them into your remote routines. Shared meals are a good first step.

## 4. Monthly Playful Icebreaker Sessions for a Team Spread Across Countries

On a team that spans several countries, getting to know one another is hard. Differences in time zones, languages, and cultures can leave colleagues feeling like strangers who happen to share a Slack channel. One of our leads addresses this directly, with games.

"Once a month, we get everyone together for an icebreaker session," says our Technology Lead. "No agenda, no work talk. We play games, answer silly questions, and just laugh together."

Each month brings different activities. "We've done 'two truths and a lie,' virtual scavenger hunts where people have to find something in their house in 60 seconds, and 'show and tell' where everyone shares something meaningful from their home. One month, we had everyone share a photo of their view from the window. We saw city skylines, rice fields, snowy streets. It really drove home how spread out we are, and how lucky we are to work together."

He rotates meeting times so that no region is always stuck with a late-night or early-morning slot. "It's a small thing, but it shows people that everyone's time matters equally."

The results have been significant. "People who'd never spoken before now message each other outside of work. When you've laughed with someone, it's so much easier to ask them for help or give them feedback. The collaboration just flows better."

**Try it:** Keep sessions light and inclusive, and rotate the schedule so everyone can attend. Having fun together turns out to be an effective way to build a team.

## Why Connection Matters: Trust, Retention, and Productivity

These tactics look small, but their effects are large. Remote team members who feel connected benefit in several ways.

**Trust grows.** People who know each other beyond their job titles are more willing to share ideas, ask for help, and give honest feedback. Every high-performing team rests on trust, and connection is how trust develops.

**Retention improves.** Employees seldom leave companies where they feel valued and seen. People with real relationships with colleagues and managers are much more likely to stay, even when other job offers arrive.

**Productivity rises.** Engaged employees put more energy, creativity, and care into their work. Teams that communicate openly solve problems faster and work together more smoothly.

Investing in connection is kind, and it is also a sound business decision.
