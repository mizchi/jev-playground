# Turn It Up: Our International Women's Day Playlist

The women who shape us leave a mark that's hard to name. It's the mother who taught us to stand tall, the grandmother whose stories we still tell, the daughter who shows us what fearless looks like, and the friend who picks up the phone at midnight without asking why. Sisterhood is more than a word: it connects generations of women through laughter, heartbreak, and everything else.

International Women's Day gives us a reason to celebrate that connection. Still, we believe womanhood deserves honoring every day, in how we support each other, how kindly we talk to ourselves, and the small moments when we choose to take up space.

This year's theme, **#BalanceforBetter**, asks for a more gender-balanced world in boardrooms, government, and the media, and in music as well. Men still overwhelmingly dominate the music industry. A major study from the USC Annenberg Inclusion Initiative looked at hundreds of popular songs from recent years and found that only around one in five artists were women, while women made up a tiny fraction of producers. Behind the scenes, the gap is even wider.

We want to help even things out a little. Our playlist gathers songs by remarkable women artists, anthems that have seen women through breakups, breakthroughs, and everything else. It runs in chronological order, taking you from 1960s soul and country to the pop powerhouses of the 2010s.

Pour yourself something delicious, put on something that feels wonderful, and press play.

## The 1960s: Finding Our Voice

**Lesley Gore – "You Don't Own Me" (1963)**
Years before young women routinely declared their independence on the radio, Lesley Gore did it with poise and plenty of nerve.
*Standout lyric:* "You don't own me, don't try to change me in any way."

**Nancy Sinatra – "These Boots Are Made for Walkin'" (1966)**
Sassy, stomping, and impossible to forget, this is for every woman who knows her worth and will walk away if she has to.
*Standout lyric:* "These boots are made for walkin', and that's just what they'll do."

**Aretha Franklin – "Respect" (1967)**
The Queen of Soul turned this song into an anthem for women and for the civil rights movement. It still gives us chills.
*Standout lyric:* "R-E-S-P-E-C-T, find out what it means to me."

**Loretta Lynn – "Don't Come Home A-Drinkin' (With Lovin' on Your Mind)" (1966)**
The coal miner's daughter said exactly what she meant. Loretta sang frankly about real women's lives, and country radio couldn't ignore her.
*Standout lyric:* "Don't come home a-drinkin' with lovin' on your mind."

**Dolly Parton – "Just Because I'm a Woman" (1968)**
Even early in her career, Dolly challenged double standards with grace and grit. Naturally, she shows up on this list a second time.
*Standout lyric:* "Just because I'm a woman."

## The 1970s: Hear Us Roar

**Helen Reddy – "I Am Woman" (1972)**
Close to being the women's movement's theme song, it's strength without apology.
*Standout lyric:* "I am woman, hear me roar."

**Gloria Gaynor – "I Will Survive" (1978)**
Few songs suit a girls' night in better. It's resilience with a beat.
*Standout lyric:* "Oh no, not I, I will survive."

## The 1980s: Doing It for Ourselves

**Dolly Parton – "9 to 5" (1980)**
Here's Dolly again, this time handing working women a clever, catchy anthem about ambition and being underestimated, set to her iconic typewriter-inspired rhythm.
*Standout lyric:* "Workin' 9 to 5, what a way to make a livin'."

**Cyndi Lauper – "Girls Just Want to Have Fun" (1983)**
Cyndi understood that joy can be radical. The song celebrates freedom, friendship, and letting go.
*Standout lyric:* "Oh, girls just want to have fun."

**Eurythmics & Aretha Franklin – "Sisters Are Doin' It for Themselves" (1985)**
Annie Lennox and Aretha on one track make for as powerful a pairing as you'll find, and a true sisterhood anthem.
*Standout lyric:* "Sisters are doin' it for themselves."

## The 1990s: Just a Girl (and So Much More)

**No Doubt – "Just a Girl" (1995)**
Gwen Stefani's pointed, sarcastic look at what's expected of young women hasn't aged a day.
*Standout lyric:* "'Cause I'm just a girl, oh, little old me."

**Shania Twain – "Man! I Feel Like a Woman!" (1997)**
Crank the volume, sing along, and have a little fun without apologizing. It's confidence set to music.
*Standout lyric:* "Man! I feel like a woman!"

**Spice Girls – "Wannabe" (1996)**
This is the song that took girl power global, with a playful reminder that the right partner has to love your friends too.
*Standout lyric:* "If you wanna be my lover, you have got to get with my friends."

## The 2000s: Survivors and Fighters

**Destiny's Child – "Survivor" (2001)**
Every woman has been through something, and this song tells us we come out the other side stronger.
*Standout lyric:* "I'm a survivor, I'm not gon' give up."

**Christina Aguilera – "Beautiful" (2002)**
Tender and soaring, it asks us to go easy on ourselves. We love passing it along to the young women in our lives.
*Standout lyric:* "I am beautiful no matter what they say."

## The 2010s: Run the World

**Beyoncé – "Run the World (Girls)" (2011)**
Bold and fierce, it's impossible to sit still through. Queen Bey gives women everywhere a rallying cry.
*Standout lyric:* "Who run the world? Girls!"

**Alicia Keys – "Girl on Fire" (2012)**
This is for the woman just starting out and for the one who has been blazing trails for decades.
*Standout lyric:* "This girl is on fire."

**Katy Perry – "Roar" (2013)**
An upbeat anthem about finding your voice after staying silent too long.
*Standout lyric:* "You're gonna hear me roar."

**Rachel Platten – "Fight Song" (2015)**
Strength sometimes begins as a whisper, and this song shows how small voices can make big waves.
*Standout lyric:* "This is my fight song."

## Keep the Music Playing

Whether you spend International Women's Day with your girlfriends, on the phone with your mom, or enjoying a quiet evening at home, we hope these songs add some joy and power to it.

Don't stop when the day ends. Send this playlist to the women who inspire you, add your own favorites, dance around your kitchen, and sing too loud in the car.

Above all, turn the music up and feel your strength. You've earned it.

Happy International Women's Day from all of us at Julianna Rae.
