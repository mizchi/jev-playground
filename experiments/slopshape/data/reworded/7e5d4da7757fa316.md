# How to Get Your Own Trucking Authority: A Step-by-Step Guide

Many drivers reach a stage where working for someone else no longer satisfies them. They want to pick their loads, set their own schedule, and keep a bigger share of what they earn. Running your own trucking operation gives you that independence, but to haul freight for hire across state lines, you first need operating authority from the federal government.

With all its acronyms and forms, the process can look intimidating. In practice, it is clear and manageable if you handle one step at a time. Here is what to do.

## Step 1: Set Up Your Business

Decide on your business structure before you file anything with the government. Many owner-operators form an LLC or corporation so that business liabilities stay separate from personal assets. Register the business with your state, get an Employer Identification Number (EIN) from the IRS, and open a business bank account. With these basics in place, every later step goes more smoothly.

## Step 2: Register With the FMCSA

The Federal Motor Carrier Safety Administration (FMCSA) regulates commercial trucking in the U.S. You now register online through the FMCSA's Unified Registration System (URS) portal.

### File Form OP-1

The OP-1 is the application for motor carrier operating authority. On it, you tell the FMCSA what kind of operation you run, for example whether you are a for-hire carrier of general freight, household goods, or passengers. Fill it out carefully and accurately, because errors can hold up your application.

### File Form BOC-3

The BOC-3 names a process agent in each state where you operate. A process agent is a person or company that can accept legal documents on your behalf. Most carriers pay a modest one-time fee for a blanket process agent service that covers all 50 states. The process agent usually files the BOC-3 electronically for you.

## Step 3: Get Your USDOT and MC Numbers

Completing your registration gets you two key identifiers:

- **USDOT Number:** This number identifies your company and tracks your safety record, inspections, and compliance. Every commercial vehicle in interstate commerce needs one.
- **MC Number:** Your Motor Carrier number grants you operating authority to haul regulated freight for hire across state lines.

You receive your USDOT number immediately, but your MC authority stays inactive until you meet the remaining requirements.

## Step 4: Buy the Right Insurance

Insurance is among the largest costs for a new carrier, and you cannot skip it. The FMCSA sets minimum levels of public liability coverage, typically $750,000 for general freight, though many brokers and shippers expect at least $1,000,000. You will probably want cargo insurance and physical damage coverage too, and possibly bobtail or non-trucking liability.

Your insurer has to file proof of coverage with the FMCSA, usually on Form BMC-91 or BMC-91X. Compare quotes from several providers and work with an agent who knows trucking. New authorities often pay higher premiums at the start, so plan your budget for that.

## Step 5: Set Up IRP Registration

To run in more than one state, you need International Registration Plan (IRP) registration. Through your home state, IRP lets you pay registration fees to multiple states and Canadian provinces in proportion to the miles you drive in each. You receive apportioned plates and a cab card that lists the jurisdictions where you can operate.

## Step 6: Open an IFTA Account

The International Fuel Tax Agreement (IFTA) makes fuel tax reporting simpler for interstate carriers. You file one quarterly report with your base state instead of filing fuel taxes in every state, and the base state passes the taxes on to the other jurisdictions. You also receive IFTA decals for your truck. Record the miles you drive and the fuel you buy in each state in detail, so reporting goes smoothly.

## Step 7: Enroll in a Drug and Alcohol Testing Program

Everyone who operates a commercial motor vehicle that requires a CDL must belong to a DOT-compliant drug and alcohol testing program, including one-truck operations. Owner-operators usually join a consortium or third-party administrator (TPA) that runs random testing pools. You also have to register with the FMCSA Drug and Alcohol Clearinghouse and pass a pre-employment drug test before you drive under your own authority.

## Step 8: Pay the Fee and Track Your Approval

Each type of authority you apply for carries a filing fee, currently $300 per authority. After you pay and submit everything, the FMCSA reviews your application.

The FMCSA's SAFER (Safety and Fitness Electronic Records) system lets you track your authority's status. Search for your USDOT or MC number to see where your application stands.

## Plan Ahead: How Long Does It Take?

Here you will need patience. Once you submit your application, a mandatory protest period begins, usually around 10 business days, when anyone can object to your authority being granted. The FMCSA also has to receive and process your insurance and BOC-3 filings.

Most new carriers should plan on three to six weeks in total, and longer if errors or backlogs come up. Keep your current job and hold off on booking loads until your authority is officially active. While you wait, line up brokers, set up your factoring or accounting systems, and prepare your truck.

## You Can Do This

Getting your trucking authority costs paperwork, money, and patience, yet thousands of drivers manage it every year. Work through the steps in order, double-check your forms, and keep good records. Soon you will be driving as your own boss.
