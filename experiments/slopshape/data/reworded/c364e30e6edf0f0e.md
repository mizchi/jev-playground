# 4 Overlooked Ways to Improve Member Experience (and Why Your Star Ratings Will Thank You)

Health plans don't have a reputation for delighting their customers. Ask people to name a great customer experience and they'll mention the coffee shop that remembers their order or the online retailer that ships overnight. Few will name their health plan.

That matters, because member experience is a hard metric, not a soft extra. It drives satisfaction scores, influences CAHPS results, shapes Medicare Advantage star ratings, and in the end affects retention, bonus payments, and the bottom line. Members who feel supported tend to stay, take part in preventive care, and avoid the emergency room.

Plenty of organizations are still catching up on customer-centered practices. Some of the most effective improvements, though, aren't the obvious ones. Here are four less obvious levers worth pulling.

## 1. Personalize Benefits Around the Whole Person

A member is more than a set of diagnosis codes. Jobs, families, stress, and living situations all weigh heavily on health. A member with diabetes who lives alone and can't get to the grocery store needs something very different from a member with the same diagnosis and a supportive family nearby.

Whole-person care looks past clinical needs to the social determinants of health: food access, transportation, housing, loneliness, and caregiving demands. Members notice when their benefits account for those conditions.

**Tactics to try:**

- **Ask better questions during onboarding.** A health risk assessment can collect more than medical history. Add questions about social support, transportation, and daily challenges.
- **Use data to segment members by need, not just demographics.** Two 70-year-olds can lead very different lives.
- **Offer flexible supplemental benefits.** Meal delivery, companionship, help around the house, and transportation can do more good than another brochure.
- **Address loneliness directly.** Social isolation is linked to worse health outcomes and higher costs. Programs like Papa pair members with Papa Pals, who offer one-on-one companionship and help with everyday tasks.
- **Support caregivers, too.** Family caregivers often manage a member's care, and giving them resources helps everyone involved.

## 2. Help Members Decode Their Coverage

Deductibles, coinsurance, out-of-pocket maximums, prior authorization, explanation of benefits: people in health insurance use these terms without thinking. To most members, they read like ancient Greek.

Confused members get frustrated. When they don't understand their coverage, they are more likely to skip care, run up unexpected bills, and call your service center already irritated. Clear explanations cost little and are one of the most underrated ways to improve experience.

**Tactics to try:**

- **Rewrite member materials in plain language.** Target a reading level most people can handle comfortably. A summary of benefits that needs a glossary needs a rewrite.
- **Use real-life examples.** Show members what a typical doctor's visit, prescription, or ER trip will cost under their plan.
- **Proactively explain changes.** When benefits or formularies change at the start of the year, contact members before they discover the change at the pharmacy counter.
- **Train service reps to teach, not just answer.** A quick explanation of how a deductible works can head off five later calls.
- **Create short videos or visual guides.** A 60-second explainer sometimes works better than a 40-page PDF. (Shocking, we know.)

## 3. Pair Digital Tools With Human Support

Many organizations assume a "modern" member experience means steering everyone to an app. Digital tools are valuable, and many members love them. Many others, especially older adults, would rather call and speak to a real person, and even digital natives want a human when a problem gets complicated.

The right approach is neither digital-first nor phone-first. It is member-first, offering options that suit each person.

**Tactics to try:**

- **Offer multiple channels and make them consistent.** Members should get the same accurate answer from the app, the website, chat, or the phone.
- **Don't bury the phone number.** Hiding human support to boost digital adoption usually hurts satisfaction.
- **Help members learn digital tools.** Some members would use the portal if someone showed them how. Papa Pals can help members get comfortable with telehealth visits, apps, and online portals.
- **Design for different generations.** Younger members may prefer text reminders and self-service, while older members may appreciate a friendly call. Let members choose their preferences.
- **Use digital data to trigger human outreach.** A member who hasn't refilled a prescription or logged in for months is due a personal check-in.

## 4. Get Creative With Preventive Care Engagement

No one disputes that preventive care saves money and improves outcomes. Annual wellness visits, screenings, vaccines, and chronic condition management form the core of quality measures. Getting members to actually come in, though, is still hard.

Reminder letters alone aren't working. Engaging members in preventive care requires more imagination and a much better grasp of the barriers they face.

**Tactics to try:**

- **Remove practical barriers.** Lack of transportation is among the most common reasons people miss appointments. Papa can provide rides to and from appointments, with a friendly companion along for the trip.
- **Bring care closer to home.** In-home screenings, mobile clinics, and telehealth visits make saying yes easier.
- **Use incentives thoughtfully.** Gift cards and rewards can prompt action, particularly when they're easy to redeem.
- **Leverage trusted messengers.** A reminder from a companion, community health worker, or familiar voice often counts for more than a form letter.
- **Make it social.** Community events, group wellness activities, and friendly challenges can make preventive care something people look forward to instead of a chore. The colonoscopy may be a stretch, but a flu shot clinic with snacks? Absolutely.
- **Close the loop.** When a member completes a screening, follow up with results, next steps, and a thank-you.

## Small Changes, Big Results

Better member experience doesn't always call for a massive overhaul or a new platform. Small human touches often matter most: a clearer explanation, a friendly voice, a ride to the doctor, a visit from someone who genuinely cares.

These improvements compound. Members who feel understood and supported are more satisfied, more engaged, and more likely to stay with their plan. They get the preventive care they need, manage chronic conditions better, and avoid costly complications. The result is stronger star ratings, better quality scores, and healthier plan finances.

Caring for your members, in other words, is also one of the smartest ways to care for your business. Pick one lever, measure its impact, and expand from there. Your members, and your CFO, will be glad you did.
