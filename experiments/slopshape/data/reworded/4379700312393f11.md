# Mocha Latte Caramel Cookie Bars: All the Crunch, None of the Added Sugar

Some afternoons, only a candy bar will do: the layered kind that's crunchy, chewy, and chocolatey at once, built on a buttery cookie base with a band of soft caramel and a chocolate shell that snaps. What comes after the last bite is the problem, because all that sugar ends in a crash.

So we built a better one. Our homemade version keeps the classic's layers but swaps in almond flour, creamy nut butter, and sugar-free dark chocolate, and each layer gets a splash of Super Coffee Mocha Latte. The result is a bar with deep coffee-chocolate flavor, a little more protein, and a place in a low-carb diet. It's a dessert that does some work for you.

## Recipe Card

**Prep time:** 25 minutes
**Cook time:** 15 minutes
**Chill time:** About 1 hour (freezer, between layers)
**Total time:** About 1 hour 40 minutes
**Yield:** 16 bars (8x8-inch pan)

### Ingredients

**Layer 1: Almond Flour Shortbread**
- 1 ½ cups blanched almond flour
- 3 tablespoons coconut flour
- 3 tablespoons granulated monk fruit or allulose sweetener
- ¼ teaspoon fine sea salt
- 4 tablespoons unsalted butter, melted
- 2 tablespoons Super Coffee Mocha Latte
- ½ teaspoon vanilla extract

**Layer 2: Nut-Butter Caramel**
- ½ cup smooth, unsweetened almond butter or cashew butter
- 3 tablespoons coconut oil, melted
- 3 tablespoons allulose syrup (or sugar-free maple-style syrup)
- 3 tablespoons Super Coffee Mocha Latte
- ½ teaspoon vanilla extract
- Pinch of flaky sea salt

**Layer 3: Sugar-Free Dark Chocolate Coating**
- 6 ounces sugar-free dark chocolate chips or chopped bar
- 1 tablespoon coconut oil
- 1 tablespoon Super Coffee Mocha Latte
- Flaky sea salt, for finishing (optional)

### Instructions

**Make the shortbread**

1. Heat the oven to 350°F. Line an 8x8-inch baking pan with parchment, letting it hang over two sides so you have handles for lifting the bars out later.
2. Whisk the almond flour, coconut flour, sweetener, and salt together in a medium bowl.
3. Stir in the melted butter, 2 tablespoons Mocha Latte, and vanilla. The mixture is ready when it resembles damp sand and clumps when you pinch it.
4. Press the dough into the bottom of the pan in a firm, even layer; a measuring cup with a flat bottom makes this easy.
5. Bake 12 to 15 minutes, until the edges turn golden. Cool on the counter for 10 minutes, then move the pan to the freezer for 15 minutes so the crust firms up.

**Make the nut-butter caramel**

6. Put the almond butter, coconut oil, allulose syrup, and 3 tablespoons Mocha Latte in a small saucepan over low heat. Stir without stopping for 2 to 3 minutes, until the mixture turns smooth and glossy and thickens slightly.
7. Take the pan off the heat and stir in the vanilla and a pinch of sea salt.
8. Pour the caramel onto the chilled shortbread and spread it evenly.
9. Put the pan back in the freezer for 30 minutes, until the caramel feels firm.

**Coat with chocolate**

10. Combine the chocolate, coconut oil, and 1 tablespoon Mocha Latte in a microwave-safe bowl. Heat in 20-second bursts, stirring after each one, until the chocolate is completely melted and smooth. (Add the coffee gradually as you stir; the coconut oil is what stops the chocolate from seizing.)
11. Pour the chocolate onto the caramel and tilt the pan until it covers the surface to the edges.
12. For a salty-sweet finish, scatter a little flaky sea salt on top.
13. Freeze 15 to 20 minutes, until the chocolate sets.
14. Use the parchment to lift the slab from the pan. Cut it into 16 bars with a sharp knife that you've warmed under hot water and wiped dry.

**Storage:** Store the bars in an airtight container in the refrigerator for up to 1 week or in the freezer for up to 2 months. If they're frozen, give them 5 minutes at room temperature before eating so the caramel softens to its best chew.

## Why Mocha Latte Makes These Bars

Coffee and chocolate are a long-standing pairing, and putting Mocha Latte in every layer does more than add caffeine. It deepens the toasted, nutty taste of the almond flour in the shortbread. In the caramel, it contributes a roasted, nearly toffee-like richness that offsets the nut butter. And in the coating, it pulls out the darker notes of the cocoa, so the bars taste like something from a café, not a health-food substitute.
