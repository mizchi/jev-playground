# Choosing the Right Mobile Devices for Your Hospital's Clinical Mobility Initiative

Clinical mobility is no longer optional; it is now central to how hospitals deliver care. When nurses, physicians, pharmacists, and support staff can communicate, pull up patient information, and finish tasks at the point of care, workflows speed up, errors fall, and clinicians get more time with patients.

Whether a clinical mobility initiative succeeds, though, depends largely on one choice: which devices your staff will carry. The right choice gives you a platform that supports care for years. The wrong one leaves you with frustrated users, security gaps, and expensive replacements.

Based on Zebra Technologies' guidance for selecting healthcare devices, these are the questions hospital administrators should ask.

## Which Device Class Is Best Suited for Clinical Settings?

Hospitals can choose among consumer smartphones, tablets, and purpose-built enterprise mobile computers. Enterprise-grade healthcare mobile computers fit most clinical workflows best.

Zebra's healthcare mobile computers and similar devices are built for clinical environments. They pair a smartphone's familiar touchscreen with the features clinicians need: integrated barcode scanning for medication administration and specimen collection, push-to-talk and voice calling, and enough durability to survive drops and constant cleaning.

Tablets are useful for particular tasks like reviewing imaging or educating patients. For staff who carry a device for their whole shift, however, a pocket-sized, healthcare-ready mobile computer usually offers the most value.

## What Selection Criteria Matter Most?

Each hospital's needs differ, but several criteria belong at the top of any evaluation.

### Security for Privacy Compliance

Healthcare mobile devices handle protected health information, so security is essential to HIPAA compliance. Look for devices that provide:

- Regular, long-term security updates and patches
- Support for encryption and secure authentication
- Integration with mobile device management (MDM) platforms
- The ability to lock down devices to approved apps and settings

Zebra devices, for instance, come with security enhancements and extended operating system update support, which keeps hospitals protected for the device's full life.

### Disinfectant-Resistant Sealing

Infection prevention can't be compromised, so devices have to hold up to frequent cleaning with your facility's hospital-grade disinfectants. Consumer devices often break down under that routine, developing cracked housings, damaged screens, and failing ports.

Healthcare-specific devices have disinfectant-ready housings and sealed designs that keep out moisture and chemicals. Always check that a device works with your approved cleaning agents.

### Voice Features

Clinical mobility depends on communication. Choose devices with strong voice capabilities: clear audio, push-to-talk, integration with nurse call and alarm systems, and support for your hospital's communication platform. When staff can receive critical alerts and call a colleague directly on one device, response times can improve dramatically.

## How Should You Map Current Communication and Legacy Tools?

Before you pick hardware, examine closely how your staff communicates and works now. Zebra advises hospitals to map their current workflows so they know what the devices must support.

Ask questions such as:

- What devices are staff carrying now? Pagers, overhead paging, desk phones, legacy wireless phones, personal smartphones?
- How are alerts and alarms delivered?
- Which clinical applications do staff use, and where?
- Where are communication breakdowns or delays happening?

Working through these questions shows which tools a new device can replace and which integrations you'll need. It also involves clinical stakeholders from the start, which helps adoption.

## Why Do Enterprise Devices Beat Consumer Phones and BYOD?

Consumer smartphones and bring-your-own-device (BYOD) programs can seem appealing because they cost less upfront. Once you account for total cost of ownership (TCO), though, enterprise devices usually win.

Consumer devices and BYOD carry hidden costs:

- **Shorter lifecycles:** Consumer phones get replaced often and models change frequently, which makes standardization hard.
- **Durability issues:** Drops and harsh disinfectants cause more repairs and replacements.
- **Security risks:** Personal devices are harder to secure and manage, which raises compliance risk.
- **Support burden:** A mix of devices and operating systems adds to IT's workload.
- **Missing features:** Consumer phones don't have integrated enterprise-grade scanning or other healthcare-specific capabilities.

Enterprise devices are built for longer service lives, stable product availability, extended OS support, and rugged construction. Across several years, those strengths mean lower total costs and fewer disruptions.

## Planning a Phased Rollout and Device Management

A strong device choice still needs a careful deployment plan. Rolling out in phases, beginning with a pilot unit, lets you test workflows, collect feedback, and make changes before expanding hospital-wide.

Device management matters just as much. With an MDM solution, IT teams can configure, update, secure, and monitor devices remotely. Work out how devices will be charged, stored, assigned, and tracked between shifts, and plan for ongoing support and training.
