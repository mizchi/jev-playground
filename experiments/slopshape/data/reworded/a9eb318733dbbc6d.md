# Closing the Preventive Care Gap: Why Navigation Support Is the Missing Link for Medicare Advantage Members

Medicare Advantage plans have put substantial money into preventive benefits. Members pay nothing for the Annual Wellness Visit (AWV), screenings are easy to get, and outreach campaigns prompt members to book appointments. Even so, AWV completion rates at many plans stay low.

Plan administrators may find this hard to explain. When a benefit is free and clearly valuable, why don't more members use it?

The cause has less to do with the benefit than with the day-to-day circumstances of older adults. For many members, getting from "you're eligible" to "you've completed your visit" means clearing one obstacle after another. Reminders alone won't close that gap; members need navigation.

## The Real Barriers to Preventive Care

**Underestimating the value of prevention.** Plenty of older adults visit the doctor only when they have a problem, so if they feel fine, an annual wellness visit can look pointless. Some mistake the AWV for a full physical and decide they don't need it, and others don't understand how preventive care can find problems before they become serious.

**Physical and cognitive limitations.** Limited mobility, chronic pain, vision or hearing loss, and early cognitive decline can all make it hard to schedule and get to appointments. A member who has trouble remembering appointments, or who can no longer drive, may let preventive care lapse without telling anyone.

**Confusion over benefits.** Medicare Advantage plans often offer many benefits, yet members may not know what's covered, what it costs, or how to use it. Worry about surprise bills stops many members from getting care they're entitled to at no charge.

**Limited digital literacy.** Health care is moving online through patient portals, appointment scheduling apps, and telehealth, and members who are uneasy with technology can fall behind. Many older adults don't have dependable internet access or the confidence to use digital tools without help.

## The Cost of Missed Prevention

These barriers cost more than convenience. Skipped preventive visits often leave chronic conditions such as diabetes, hypertension, heart disease, and COPD undiagnosed or poorly controlled. Minor, treatable problems then grow into complications that lead to emergency care, hospital admissions, and readmissions.

Plans feel a significant financial effect. Avoidable hospitalizations and emergency visits raise medical costs, and gaps in preventive care hurt quality measures and Star Ratings, which then affect reimbursement and member enrollment. Members pay in worsening health, lost independence, and a lower quality of life.

## How Companion Care Closes the Gap

Companion care professionals give members personal, hands-on help that connects the benefit as designed with how it gets used in practice. As a supplemental benefit, companion care addresses the particular barriers that keep members from preventive care.

**Helping members understand their coverage.** A companion can explain a member's benefits in plain language, including that the AWV is covered and what happens during the visit. That one conversation can calm worries about cost and give the member confidence to move forward.

**Scheduling appointments.** Instead of members working through phone trees and online portals by themselves, companions help place calls, book visits, and track upcoming appointments with reminders.

**Arranging transportation.** Lack of transportation is still among the most frequent reasons older adults miss appointments. Companions can help arrange rides, using plan-covered transportation benefits or other resources, and sometimes go with members to their visits.

**Supporting telehealth access.** When virtual visits would help a member, companions set up devices, log in to appointments, and fix technical problems, so telehealth becomes convenient rather than frustrating.

**Serving as the plan's eyes and ears.** Since companions see members regularly, they often pick up on changes others overlook: new trouble walking, confusion about medications, an empty refrigerator, or signs of loneliness and depression. Given the right communication channels, they can pass these observations to the plan, allowing care management to step in before a small issue turns into a crisis.

## Value for Plans and Members Alike

Navigation support delivered through companion care benefits both parties. Plans gain higher AWV completion rates, better chronic condition management, fewer avoidable hospitalizations, improved quality scores, and greater member satisfaction and retention.

Members gain something more personal: a person on their side who helps them understand their options, get the care they need, and remain healthy and independent at home.

Preventive care only works when members can reach it. When Medicare Advantage plans combine strong benefits with human support, they can close the gap between what's offered and what's used, and achieve better outcomes for everyone.
